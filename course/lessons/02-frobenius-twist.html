2. Frobenius and the twist X′

In characteristic \(p\) every scheme carries a canonical endomorphism for free: raise functions to the \(p\)-th power. This is the absolute Frobenius. It is too crude to be a morphism over the base, so we correct it into the relative Frobenius \(F = F_{X/S}\colon X \to X'\), which lands not in \(X\) but in a twisted copy \(X'\) — "\(X\) with its coefficients raised to the \(p\)-th power." This \(F\) is the single morphism the whole paper is about: \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is the object we will decompose, and the reason it is decomposable starts from one tiny miracle — \(F^{*}=0\) on differentials. We build \(X'\) and \(F\) carefully here, because confusing \(X\) with \(X'\) is the most common way to get lost later.

Absolute Frobenius

Let \(S\) be a scheme of characteristic \(p\): the structure map \(\cO_S \to \cO_S\), \(a \mapsto a^p\), makes sense because \((a+b)^p = a^p + b^p\) (the binomial coefficients \(\binom{p}{k}\) for \(0<k<p\) are divisible by \(p\), hence \(0\)). The absolute Frobenius \(F_S\colon S \to S\) is the identity on the underlying topological space, with \(F_S^{\sharp}\colon \cO_S \to \cO_S\) equal to \(a \mapsto a^p\). For any scheme \(X\) over \(S\) one likewise has \(F_X\colon X \to X\), the identity on points and the \(p\)-th power on functions.

The trouble: \(F_X\) is not a morphism over \(S\). The square with the two structure maps \(u\colon X\to S\) does not commute over \(\mathrm{id}_S\); rather \(u \circ F_X = F_S \circ u\). Frobenius "covers" \(F_S\), not the identity. If we want a Frobenius that respects the base — which we must, to do relative geometry — we have to twist the target. That twist is \(X'\).

The relative Frobenius and the twist \(X'\)

Definition (the twist)

For \(u\colon X \to S\) with \(S\) of characteristic \(p\), set \[ X' \;:=\; X \times_{S,\,F_S} S, \] the base change of \(X\) along the absolute Frobenius \(F_S\colon S \to S\). It is again an \(S\)-scheme (via the second projection), and there is a canonical projection \(W\colon X' \to X\) covering \(F_S\).

By the universal property of the fibre product, the two maps \(F_X\colon X\to X\) and \(u\colon X\to S\) — which satisfy exactly the compatibility \(u\circ F_X = F_S\circ u\) needed — define a unique \(S\)-morphism

\[ F = F_{X/S}\colon X \longrightarrow X', \]

the relative Frobenius, characterized by \(W\circ F = F_X\) and (projection to \(S\)) \(\circ\, F = u\). The full picture:

\[ \xymatrix{ X \ar@/^1.2pc/[rrd]^{F_X} \ar@/_1.2pc/[rdd]_{u} \ar@{-->}[rd]|{F_{X/S}} & & \\ & X' \ar[r]^{W} \ar[d] & X \ar[d]^{u} \\ & S \ar[r]^{F_S} & S } \]

The inner square is cartesian. On functions, a local section of \(\cO_{X'}\) coming from \(x \in \cO_X\) is written \(x\otimes 1\), and

\[ \tag{2.1} F^{*}(x\otimes 1) \;=\; x^{p}. \]

(Scalars from \(S\) are pulled out through the \(F_S\)-twist: \(F^{*}(a\otimes 1)=a\) for \(a\in\cO_S\), while \(x\) itself gets raised to the \(p\).)

Example — read off \(X'\) from equations

Suppose \(X \subset \Aa^n_S\) is cut out by polynomials \(f_\alpha = \sum_m a_{\alpha,m}\,T^m\) with \(a_{\alpha,m}\in\cO_S\). Then base-changing the coefficients along \(F_S\) gives \[ X' \subset \Aa^n_S, \qquad f_\alpha^{\sigma} = \sum_m a_{\alpha,m}^{\,p}\,T^m, \] i.e. the same monomials with every coefficient raised to the \(p\)-th power, and the relative Frobenius is \(F\colon T_i \mapsto T_i^{p}\) (this is (2.1) on the coordinate \(T_i\)). So "\(X'\) is \(X\) with twisted coefficients," and \(F\) is the honest \(p\)-power map on the variables. This is the mental picture to keep.

Worked examples

Example — the affine line

Let \(X=\Aa^1_k=\Spec k[t]\). There are no coefficients to twist, so \(X'=\Aa^1_k=\Spec k[t']\), and \(F\colon t' \mapsto t^{p}\). Thus \(F_*\cO_X = k[t]\) viewed as a module over \(\cO_{X'}=k[t']\) acting through \(t'\mapsto t^p\); it is free of rank \(p\) on the basis \(1, t, t^2, \dots, t^{p-1}\), since every monomial \(t^n\) is uniquely \(t^{p q + r}=(t^p)^q t^r\) with \(0\leqslant r<p\). So \(F\) is finite flat of degree \(p = p^{1}\), matching relative dimension \(1\). ✓

Example — \(\Gm\), and a twist you can see

For \(\Gm=\Spec k[t,t^{-1}]\) again \(X'=\Gm\) and \(F\colon t'\mapsto t^p\). The twist becomes visible over a non-prime field. Take an elliptic curve \(E\colon y^2 = x^3 + a x + b\) over \(k\) (char \(\neq 2,3\)). Then \[ E'\colon y^2 = x^3 + a^{p} x + b^{p}, \qquad F\colon (x,y)\mapsto (x^{p},y^{p}). \] The coefficients have genuinely moved. If \(a,b\in\Fp\) then \(a^p=a,\,b^p=b\), so \(E'=E\) on the nose — yet \(F\) is still the nontrivial map \((x,y)\mapsto(x^p,y^p)\), not the identity. The twist is in the target's structure, not always in its equations.

Basic properties of \(F\)

For \(X/S\) smooth of relative dimension \(r\), the relative Frobenius \(F_{X/S}\) is:

  • finite locally free of degree \(p^{r}\) (étale-locally \(X\) looks like \(\Aa^r\), where \(F\) is \(T_i\mapsto T_i^p\) in \(r\) variables, a free module on the \(p^r\) monomials \(T^m\) with each exponent \(<p\));
  • a universal homeomorphism, and purely inseparable — it is a bijection on points but very far from an isomorphism of schemes.

The one fact that powers everything: \(F^{*}=0\) on \(\Om^1\)

Crucial Lemma (2.2)

The pullback map on Kähler differentials \[ F^{*}\colon \Om^1_{X'/S} \longrightarrow F_*\Om^1_{X/S} \] is the zero map.

Proof, one line. The generators of \(\Om^1_{X'/S}\) are the \(d(x\otimes 1)\). Pullback commutes with \(d\), so by (2.1) \[ F^{*}\bigl(d(x\otimes 1)\bigr) = d\bigl(F^{*}(x\otimes 1)\bigr) = d(x^{p}) = p\,x^{p-1}\,dx = 0, \] because \(p=0\) in characteristic \(p\). \(\square\) The same computation works degree by degree, so \(F^*=0\) on every \(\Om^i_{X'/S}\) with \(i>0\) (see the exercises). This vanishing is the seed of the Cartier isomorphism (Lesson 3) and of the whole local computation (Lesson 6): because \(F^*\) kills differentials on the nose, its lift mod \(p^2\) will be divisible by \(p\), and dividing by \(p\) is what manufactures the splitting.

\(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is an honest \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear complex

Although \(F^*=0\), the pushforward \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is a perfectly nonzero complex of \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules — \(\cO_{X'}\) acts through \(F^{*}\), i.e. \(x\otimes 1\) acts as multiplication by \(x^p\). Its differential \(d\) is \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear: for the action element \(x^p=F^*(x\otimes 1)\), \[ d(x^{p}\,\omega) = d(x^{p})\wedge\omega + x^{p}\,d\omega = x^{p}\,d\omega \] since \(d(x^p)=0\). (Equivalently: \(d\) is \(F^{-1}\cO_{X'}\)-linear.) Over \(\cO_X\) the de Rham differential is only a connection, not linear; the miracle of characteristic \(p\) is that after pushing forward along \(F\) it becomes linear over the new structure sheaf. This is exactly what lets us treat \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) as a complex in \(D(X')\) and ask whether it splits.

Over a perfect field: \(X'\) versus \(X\)

If \(k\) is perfect, \(a\mapsto a^p\) is a bijection, so \(F_S=\Spec(\text{Frob})\) is an isomorphism of schemes. Hence \(X'=X\times_{k,\,F_k}k\) is abstractly isomorphic to \(X\) — but the isomorphism re-labels the \(k\)-structure by \(\sigma^{-1}\) (where \(\sigma=\text{Frob}\)). Concretely \(X'\) is the extension of scalars of \(X\) along the field automorphism \(\sigma^{-1}\): the underlying scheme is "the same," its \(k\)-linear structure is twisted. Since a field automorphism preserves all \(k\)-dimensions, \[ \tag{2.3} \dim_k H^j(X',\Om^i_{X'/k}) = \dim_k H^j(X,\Om^i_{X/k}) \quad\text{for all }i,j: \] \(X'\) has the same Hodge numbers as \(X\). We lean on this constantly in Lesson 9: the splitting lives on \(X'\), but the Hodge numbers we want to add up are those of \(X\), and (2.3) bridges the two.

Keep \(F\colon X\to X'\), always

Because \(X'\cong X\) abstractly over a perfect field, it is tempting to erase the prime and pretend \(F\) is an endomorphism of \(X\). Resist. First, the main theorem's lifting hypothesis is on \(X'\), not \(X\) — and "lift \(X'\) mod \(p^2\)" is the correct, slightly stronger-looking demand (Lesson 14). Second, in a family over a base \(S\) that is not a perfect field, \(F_S\) is genuinely not invertible and \(X'\) genuinely differs from \(X\) (Lesson 13). The clean, always-correct statement is: \(F\) is the relative Frobenius \(X\to X'\).

Exercises
  1. Show \(F^{*}=0\) on \(\Om^i_{X'/S}\) for every \(i\geqslant 1\), directly from \(d(x^p)=0\) and the Leibniz rule. (Reduce to monomials \(d(x_1\otimes 1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x_i\otimes 1)\).)
  2. For the conic \(C\colon a x^2 + b y^2 = c\) and for an elliptic curve of your choice over a field containing a non-\(p\)-th-power, write down \(C'\), \(E'\), and the map \(F\) explicitly, identifying the twist of the coefficients. When does \(X'=X\) as schemes?
  3. Verify directly that \(F_*\cO_{\Aa^1}\) is free of rank \(p\) over \(\cO_{X'}=k[t']\), and determine the \(\cO_{X'}\)-module structure of \(F_*\Om^1_{\Aa^1/k}\). (It is free of rank \(p\) on \(dt, t\,dt,\dots,t^{p-1}dt\); note \(t'\) acts by \(t^p\).) Check \(F^*(dt')=d(t^p)=p\,t^{p-1}dt=0\).
  4. Prove \(F_{X/S}\) has degree \(p^{r}\) for \(X/S\) smooth of relative dimension \(r\), by reducing étale-locally to \(\Aa^r_S\) and counting monomials \(T^m\) with all exponents \(<p\).
  5. Explain why the differential of \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear, and contrast with the fact that \(d\colon\cO_X\to\Om^1_X\) is not \(\cO_X\)-linear. Where exactly does \(d(x^p)=0\) enter?
