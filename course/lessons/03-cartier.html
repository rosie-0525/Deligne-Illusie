3. The Cartier isomorphism

In Lesson 2 we built the relative Frobenius \(F:X\to X'\) and saw that on differentials \(F^*=0\). The consequence is striking: the de Rham differential \(d:\Om^i_{X/S}\to\Om^{i+1}_{X/S}\), pushed forward along \(F\), is \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear, so \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is an honest complex of \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules and we may speak of its cohomology sheaves. Cartier's theorem computes them — and the answer is again a complex of differential forms, but on the twist \(X'\). This is the first of the two pillars on which Deligne–Illusie stand; it is purely characteristic \(p\), uses no lift, and we prove it in full on the affine line.

Why this is a sensible question

For a section \(x'\) of \(\cO_{X'}\) and a section \(\omega\) of \(F_*\Om^i_{X/S}\) we have \(d(F^*x'\cdot\omega)=F^*x'\cdot d\omega\), because \(d(F^*x')=F^*(dx')=0\). Thus \(d\) commutes with multiplication by \(\cO_{X'}\): each \(d\) is \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear, and \[ \cH^i F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\;:=\;\frac{\ker\bigl(d:F_*\Om^i_{X/S}\to F_*\Om^{i+1}_{X/S}\bigr)} {\operatorname{im}\bigl(d:F_*\Om^{i-1}_{X/S}\to F_*\Om^i_{X/S}\bigr)} \] is a sheaf of \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules. (Over \(\cO_X\) it would be nothing: \(d\) is not linear and the quotient is not even a sheaf of modules.) The miracle is that these subquotients are free and have a canonical description.

Theorem (1.2) (Cartier)

Let \(X\to S\) be smooth with \(S\) of characteristic \(p>0\). There is a unique homomorphism of graded \(\cO_{X'}\)-algebras \[ C^{-1}\;:\;\bigoplus_i \Om^i_{X'/S}\;\longrightarrow\;\bigoplus_i \cH^i F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S} \] such that, for every local section \(x\) of \(\cO_X\) (with \(x\otimes 1\) the corresponding section of \(\cO_{X'}\)), \[ \tag{3.1} C^{-1}\bigl(d(x\otimes 1)\bigr)\;=\;\bigl[\,x^{p-1}\,dx\,\bigr]\in\cH^1 F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}. \] Moreover \(C^{-1}\) is an isomorphism. Its inverse \(C\) is the Cartier operator.

Being a map of graded algebras, \(C^{-1}\) is multiplicative, \(C^{-1}(\alpha\wedge\beta)=C^{-1}(\alpha)\wedge C^{-1}(\beta)\); since the wedge powers of \(\Om^1\) generate the whole exterior algebra, formula (3.1) determines \(C^{-1}\) completely — hence uniqueness. The content of the theorem is that the resulting map is an isomorphism in every degree.

Degree 0: functions killed by \(d\) are \(p\)-th powers

In degree \(0\), \(\cH^0 F_*\Om^{\bullet}=\ker(d:F_*\cO_X\to F_*\Om^1)\) is the sheaf of functions with vanishing differential. Étale-locally on a smooth scheme such functions are exactly the \(p\)-th powers: \(d f=0 \iff f=g^p\) locally. The \(p\)-th powers are precisely the image of \(\cO_{X'}\hookrightarrow F_*\cO_X\), so \(\cH^0\cong\cO_{X'}\), and \(C^{-1}\) in degree \(0\) is this identification \(x'\mapsto x^p\). This is the bottom rung of the algebra map.

The affine line, computed by hand

Everything is forced by one example. Take \(X=\Aa^1_k=\Spec k[t]\), \(k\) perfect of characteristic \(p\). The twist is again \(X'=\Aa^1\) with coordinate we also call \(t\), but it maps in by \(t\mapsto t^p\); concretely \(\cO_{X'}=k[t^p]\subset k[t]=\cO_X\). As a module over \(\cO_{X'}=k[t^p]\), \(\cO_X\) is free with basis \(1,t,\dots,t^{p-1}\): \[ F_*\cO_X=\bigoplus_{0\le a\le p-1} t^a\,k[t^p], \qquad F_*\Om^1_X=\bigoplus_{0\le a\le p-1} t^a\,k[t^p]\,dt . \] The differential is \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear and on the basis it reads \[ d\bigl(t^a g(t^p)\bigr) = a\,t^{a-1}g(t^p)\,dt + t^a g'(t^p)\,\underbrace{p\,t^{p-1}}_{=\,0}\,dt = a\,t^{a-1}g(t^p)\,dt . \] The second term dies because \(p=0\): this is precisely the place where Frobenius "kills" the inner derivative. Now read off both cohomology sheaves.

Kernel (\(\cH^0\)). \(a\,t^{a-1}g(t^p)=0\) forces \(a\equiv 0\pmod p\), i.e. \(a=0\) in the range \(0\le a\le p-1\). Hence \(\ker d=k[t^p]=\cO_{X'}\), confirming \(\cH^0=\cO_{X'}\).

Cokernel (\(\cH^1\)). Consider a monomial \(t^b\,dt\). If \(b+1\not\equiv0\pmod p\) then \[ t^b\,dt=d\!\left(\frac{t^{b+1}}{b+1}\right)\qquad(b+1\ \text{invertible in } k), \] so \(t^b\,dt\) is exact. If \(b\equiv p-1\pmod p\) then \(b+1\) is divisible by \(p\), the primitive is illegal, and \(t^b\,dt\) is not exact. Writing \(b=(m+1)p-1\) gives the classes \([t^{mp}\cdot t^{p-1}\,dt]=t^{mp}\,[t^{p-1}dt]\). Therefore \[ \cH^1 F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{\Aa^1}=k[t^p]\cdot[t^{p-1}\,dt] \] is free of rank \(1\) over \(\cO_{X'}\), generated by \([t^{p-1}\,dt]\). Finally \(\Om^1_{X'}=\cO_{X'}\,dt\) and formula (3.1) with \(x=t\) gives \(C^{-1}(dt)=[t^{p-1}\,dt]\) — the generator. So \(C^{-1}\) is an isomorphism in degree \(1\) as well. \(\quad\checkmark\)

From the line to everything

The general case is now formal. \(C^{-1}\) is a map of graded \(\cO_{X'}\)-algebras, so it suffices to check it is an isomorphism in degrees \(0\) and \(1\) and that the source is generated in those degrees — which is exactly the statement that \(\bigoplus_i\Om^i_{X'}=\bigwedge^{\bullet}\Om^1_{X'}\). Both source and target are compatible with étale localization, and any smooth \(X\) is étale-locally a product of affine lines (étale-locally there are coordinates \(t_1,\dots,t_n\) with \(\Om^1\) free on \(dt_1,\dots,dt_n\)). The affine-line computation handles each coordinate; multiplicativity and Künneth assemble them.

Proof (reduction to \(\Aa^1\))

Uniqueness is the algebra remark above. For existence and bijectivity: that \(x^{p-1}dx\) is closed and that the recipe (3.1) is additive and a derivation in the appropriate sense make \(C^{-1}\) well defined as an algebra map (this is the only delicate verification; see Katz, Nilpotent connections and the monodromy theorem, 7.2, or Illusie's exposé). To check it is an isomorphism we may work étale-locally, where \(\Om^1_{X/S}\) is free on coordinates \(dt_1,\dots,dt_n\). The complex \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is then the tensor product over \(\cO_{X'}\) of the \(n\) one-variable complexes \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{\Aa^1}\). By Künneth (each factor is a complex of free modules) its cohomology is the tensor product of the factor cohomologies, and we computed each factor: \(\cO_{X'}\) in degree \(0\), \(\cO_{X'}[t_j^{p-1}dt_j]\) in degree \(1\). Taking the product reconstructs \(\bigwedge^{\bullet}\Om^1_{X'/S}=\bigoplus_i\Om^i_{X'/S}\), and the identification is exactly the multiplicative \(C^{-1}\). Hence \(C^{-1}\) is an isomorphism in all degrees. ∎

More examples

Example — the multiplicative group \(\Gm\)

Let \(X=\Gm=\Spec k[t,t^{-1}]\). The logarithmic form \(dt/t\) is closed and not exact (its would-be primitive \(\log t\) is not algebraic), so it represents a nonzero class in \(\cH^1\). Cartier fixes \(d\!\log\): writing \(t'=t\otimes 1\) for the coordinate on the twist, \[ C^{-1}\!\left(\frac{dt'}{t'}\right)=\left[\frac{dt}{t}\right]. \] Check it from (3.1): \(C^{-1}(dt')=[t^{p-1}dt]=[t^p\cdot dt/t]\); dividing by the degree-\(0\) factor \(t'\mapsto t^p\) leaves \([dt/t]\). The class \(d\!\log\) of a unit is its own Cartier image — the arithmetic shadow of the fact that \(d\!\log\) of a \(p\)-th power equals \(p\,d\!\log=0\) of the original.

Example — affine \(n\)-space

For \(X=\Aa^n=\Spec k[t_1,\dots,t_n]\), Künneth and multiplicativity give \(\cH^i F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{\Aa^n}\cong\Om^i_{X'/S}\), free over \(\cO_{X'}=k[t_1^p,\dots,t_n^p]\) with basis the classes \[ \bigl[\,t_{j_1}^{\,p-1}dt_{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge t_{j_i}^{\,p-1}dt_{j_i}\,\bigr], \qquad j_1<\cdots<j_i . \] This is the local model for every smooth \(X\): Cartier says the cohomology of the de Rham complex looks, étale-locally, exactly like the forms on the twist.

Three subtleties to carry forward

\(C^{-1}\) lives only on cohomology

\(C^{-1}\) is an isomorphism of \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules between \(\bigoplus_i\Om^i_{X'}\) and \(\bigoplus_i\cH^i F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X}\). It is emphatically not induced by a morphism of complexes \(\bigoplus_i\Om^i_{X'}[-i]\to F_*\Om^{\bullet}_X\): there is no canonical chain-level map, only the identification of subquotients. Producing an actual map in the derived category — splitting \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_X\) as the sum of its cohomology sheaves so that \(C^{-1}\) is realized by morphisms — is precisely the Deligne–Illusie theorem (Lesson 5, Lesson 6), and it requires the lift mod \(p^2\).

Why \(x^{p-1}\,dx\)? It is the obstruction to dividing by \(p\)

The form \(x^{p-1}dx\) is closed, \(d(x^{p-1}dx)=(p-1)x^{p-2}\,dx\wedge dx=0\), but not exact: a primitive would be \(x^p/p\), and the \(1/p\) is illegal in characteristic \(p\). Morally the Cartier class measures exactly "the failure to divide \(x^p\) by \(p\)." This is not a coincidence with the main theorem — it is the main theorem in embryo. In Lesson 6 a lift \(\widetilde F\) of Frobenius mod \(p^2\) satisfies \(\widetilde F^*(x)=x^p+p\,u\), so \(\tfrac1p\widetilde F^*(dx)=x^{p-1}dx+du\): the lift literally supplies the missing division by \(p\), and its class is \(x^{p-1}dx\). Cartier names the obstruction; the lift removes it.

The source is \(\Om^i_{X'}\), not \(\Om^i_X\)

\(C^{-1}\) is "Frobenius-semilinear in spirit": its source is the forms on the twist \(X'\), reflecting the \(p\)-th-power twist baked into relative Frobenius. Over a perfect field \(X'\cong X\) abstractly, so one often blurs the distinction, but in families (and for the lifting question) the appearance of \(X'\) is essential — see Lesson 13.

Exercises
  1. Redo the \(\Aa^1\) computation from scratch: write \(F_*\cO_X\) and \(F_*\Om^1_X\) as free \(k[t^p]\)-modules, compute \(d\) on the basis, and identify \(\cH^0,\cH^1\). Confirm \(C^{-1}(dt)=[t^{p-1}dt]\).
  2. Compute \(\cH^i F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{\Aa^2}\) for \(i=0,1,2\) directly, and verify that the basis classes multiply as \([t_1^{p-1}dt_1]\wedge[t_2^{p-1}dt_2]=[t_1^{p-1}t_2^{p-1}\,dt_1\wedge dt_2]\), i.e. that \(C^{-1}\) is multiplicative here.
  3. Show \(\cH^0 F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X}=\cO_{X'}\) for any smooth \(X\): use \(d(x^p)=p\,x^{p-1}dx=0\) and the fact that, étale-locally on a smooth scheme, \(\ker d=\{\text{$p$-th powers}\}\).
  4. For \(\Gm\), verify \(C^{-1}(dt'/t')=[dt/t]\) both directly (show \(dt/t\) is closed and not exact) and via the recipe (3.1).
  5. On \(\Aa^1\), explain why \([t^{p-1}dt]\ne 0\) in \(\cH^1\) while \([t^b\,dt]=0\) for every \(0\le b<p-1\). (Exhibit the explicit primitives \(t^{b+1}/(b+1)\), and argue that no primitive of \(t^{p-1}dt\) exists in \(k[t]\).)
