4. Witt vectors and liftings mod p²

The hypothesis that makes the whole machine run is almost embarrassingly small: the variety should lift one infinitesimal step, from characteristic \(p\) to "characteristic \(p^2\)". This lesson supplies the two ingredients that give that phrase a precise meaning — the ring \(\Wt(k)\) of Witt vectors of length two, the canonical \(p^2\)-thickening of a perfect field \(k\), and the deformation theory that decides which smooth \(X\) admit a lift over it. We will see that affine schemes and curves always lift, that anything defined over \(\Z\) lifts for free, but that liftability is a genuine, sometimes-violated condition. We close by lifting Frobenius itself — the precise non-uniqueness there is the seed of every homotopy in Lesson 6.

Witt vectors of a perfect field

Fix a perfect field \(k\) of characteristic \(p\). One wants a "best possible" ring of characteristic \(0\) sitting over \(k\): flat over \(\Z\), \(p\)-adically complete, with residue field \(k\). That ring exists and is unique.

Definition

The ring of Witt vectors \(\WW(k)\) is the unique (up to unique isomorphism) ring that is \(p\)-torsion-free, complete and separated for the \(p\)-adic topology, and satisfies \(\WW(k)/p\,\WW(k)\cong k\). Set \(\Wn(k):=\WW(k)/p^{n}\WW(k)\); then \(\WW(k)=\varprojlim_n \Wn(k)\). The ring \(\Wn(k)\) is characterized by being flat over \(\Z/p^{n}\) with \(\Wn(k)/p\cong k\).

Concretely, elements of \(\WW(k)\) are sequences \((a_0,a_1,a_2,\dots)\) with \(a_i\in k\), added and multiplied by universal polynomial formulas (designed so that \((a_0,a_1,\dots)\mapsto a_0\) is the reduction \(\WW(k)\to k\)). For our purposes the formulas matter less than the two characterizing properties — flatness and the residue field — which already pin the ring down.

The Frobenius \(a\mapsto a^{p}\) of \(k\) lifts canonically to a ring automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(\WW(k)\) (and of each \(\Wn(k)\)), given in coordinates by \[ \sigma(a_0,a_1,a_2,\dots)=(a_0^{\,p},a_1^{\,p},a_2^{\,p},\dots). \] This \(\sigma\) reduces mod \(p\) to the Frobenius of \(k\); it is the algebraic incarnation of the twist \(X\mapsto X'\) from Lesson 2, and it will reappear in every base change below.

Examples — the rings you must keep in mind

(a) Prime field. \(\WW(\Fp)=\Z_p\), the \(p\)-adic integers, and \(\Wt(\Fp)=\Z/p^{2}\). Here \(\sigma=\mathrm{id}\) (Frobenius is the identity on \(\Fp\)).
(b) Finite fields. For \(q=p^{f}\), \(\WW(\mathbf{F}_q)\) is the unramified extension of \(\Z_p\) of degree \(f\), and \(\sigma\) is its arithmetic Frobenius (the generator of the Galois group). E.g. \(\Wt(\mathbf{F}_{p^2})=\mathbf{Z}_{p^2}/p^2\), the length-two truncation of the unramified quadratic extension; here \(\sigma\neq\mathrm{id}\).
(c) \(\Wt(k)\) is the canonical characteristic-\(p^2\) thickening of \(k\): a ring of length two over itself, with \(\Wt(k)/p\cong k\).

Liftings over a square-zero thickening

Let \(\tS\supset S\) be a closed immersion of schemes defined by a square-zero ideal \(I\) (so \(I^{2}=0\)).

Definition

For \(X\) flat over \(S\), a lift of \(X\) over \(\tS\) is a flat \(\tS\)-scheme \(\tX\) together with an isomorphism \(\tX\times_{\tS}S\iso X\). If \(X/S\) is smooth, every lift \(\tX\) is automatically smooth over \(\tS\).

The case of interest is \(S=\Spec k\), \(\tS=\Spec \Wt(k)\). In \(\Wt(k)\) we have \(p\cdot p=p^{2}=0\), so the ideal \((p)\) is square-zero; and multiplication by \(p\) gives an isomorphism of \(\cO_X=k\)-modules \(k=\Wt(k)/p\iso p\,\Wt(k)\). Thus lifting over \(\Wt(k)\) is a square-zero deformation problem whose ideal is the structure sheaf itself, \(I\cong\cO_X\). To "lift \(X\) mod \(p^{2}\)" means precisely: find a flat \(\Wt(k)\)-scheme \(\tX\) with \(\tX\times_{\Wt(k)}k\cong X\).

Deformation theory: who lifts, and how many ways

Let \(X/k\) be smooth, with tangent sheaf \(\Theta=T_X=\Hom_{\cO_X}(\Om^{1}_{X/k},\cO_X)\). The square-zero deformation problem with ideal \(\cO_X\) is governed by the coherent cohomology of \(\Theta\). This is the structural input the paper uses (e.g. in §3, Lesson 12).

Deformation theorem

For \(X/k\) smooth:

  • the obstruction to the existence of a lift over \(\Wt(k)\) lies in \(H^{2}(X,\Theta_{X/k})\);
  • if a lift exists, the set of isomorphism classes of lifts is a torsor (an affine space) under \(H^{1}(X,\Theta_{X/k})\);
  • the automorphisms of a fixed lift inducing the identity on \(X\) form \(H^{0}(X,\Theta_{X/k})\).

So liftability is purely cohomological: an obstruction in \(H^2\), an ambiguity measured by \(H^1\), symmetries by \(H^0\). Smoothness of the lift is automatic; nothing more than these two groups is at stake.

Examples — which X lift

Affine smooth. If \(X\) is affine, \(\Theta\) is quasicoherent so \(H^{\geqslant 1}(X,\Theta)=0\): a lift always exists and is unique up to (non-unique) isomorphism. In particular \(\mathbf{A}^{n}_k\) lifts to \(\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\Wt(k)}\).
Curves. If \(\dim X=1\) then \(H^{2}(X,\Theta)=0\) for dimension reasons, so every smooth curve lifts.
Things defined over \(\Z\). \(\Pp^{n}\), and any smooth complete intersection in \(\Pp^{n}\), have evident lifts (base-change the model over \(\Z\) to \(\Wt(k)\)).
Abelian varieties. always lift (e.g. the canonical Serre–Tate lift).

Not everything lifts

It is a genuine condition. There exist smooth projective varieties — Mumford's, Igusa's, and Lang's surfaces — that do not lift mod \(p^{2}\); they are detected precisely by the failure of Hodge–de Rham degeneration (Lesson 14). The existence of such examples is what gives the main theorem content: liftability is not free, and when it holds it buys you something real.

Lifting Frobenius

Now suppose a lift \(\tX/\Wt(k)\) is given. Base-change along \(\sigma:\Wt(k)\to\Wt(k)\) to get \(\tX':=\tX\times_{\sigma}\Wt(k)\), a lift of the Frobenius twist \(X'\). The question that powers Lesson 6 is whether the relative Frobenius \(F:X\to X'\) lifts to a \(\Wt(k)\)-morphism \(\tF:\tX\to\tX'\).

Local yes, global usually no

On affine opens \(\tF\) always exists, by smoothness of \(\tX'\) and the lifting property along the square-zero immersion. Globally the local lifts need not agree: the obstruction to a global \(\tF\) lives in \(H^{1}(X',\Theta_{X'}\otimes F_*\cO_X)\) (Lesson 8). Moreover any two lifts of \(F\) differ by \(p\cdot(\text{a derivation})\) — this exact \(p\)-divisible discrepancy is what gets divided by \(p\) to produce the Cartier-type splitting map.

Examples — Frobenius on \(\mathbf{A}^1\) and an elliptic curve

The affine line. Take \(\tX=\Spec\Wt(k)[t]\), so \(\tX'=\mathbf{A}^1_{\Wt(k)}\) too. One lift of \(F\) is \(\tF\colon t\mapsto t^{p}\). But for any polynomial \(g\), \(\tF_g\colon t\mapsto t^{p}+p\,g(t)\) is another lift (it reduces to \(t\mapsto t^p\) mod \(p\)). The difference of two lifts, \[ \tF_g^{*}-\tF^{*} = p\,g\cdot\tfrac{d}{dt}, \] is \(p\) times the derivation \(g\,\partial_t\) — explicitly confirming "two lifts differ by \(p\cdot(\text{derivation})\)". This non-uniqueness is the source of the homotopies of Lessons 6 and 8.
An elliptic curve \(E/k\). Here \(T_E\cong\cO_E\) (the tangent bundle is trivial), so the lifts of \(E\) form a torsor under \(H^{1}(E,T_E)=H^{1}(E,\cO_E)=k\): a one-parameter family. The canonical (Serre–Tate) lift is a distinguished point of this line.

Why mod p², and why X' rather than X

(i) Why exactly \(p^{2}\)? The construction in Lesson 6 divides by \(p\) exactly once, so it only needs the lifted equation to be defined modulo \(p^{2}\). Mod \(p\) alone gives nothing (\(F^{*}=0\)); higher \(\Wn\) or all of \(\WW(k)\) is unnecessary for the main theorems — it yields only refinements (the Witt-complex variants).
(ii) It is \(X'\) that must lift. Strictly the hypothesis is a lift of the twist \(X'\). Over a perfect field \(X\cong X'\) abstractly, so the distinction is invisible here; but in families and in the logarithmic theory it is real (Lesson 13, Lesson 14).

Exercises
  1. Show \(\Wt(\Fp)=\Z/p^{2}\) and that \(\sigma=\mathrm{id}\) on it. (Use the characterization: flat over \(\Z/p^{2}\), residue field \(\Fp\).)
  2. Prove that an affine smooth \(X/k\) admits a lift over \(\Wt(k)\), unique up to isomorphism, and that every smooth curve lifts. (Compute the cohomological dimension of \(\Theta\) in each case.)
  3. For \(F:\mathbf{A}^1\to\mathbf{A}^1\), write down all lifts \(\tF\) explicitly and verify that any two differ by \(p\cdot(\text{a derivation})\); identify the derivation in terms of the polynomial \(g\).
  4. Describe the torsor of lifts of an ordinary elliptic curve \(E/k\): identify the group \(H^{1}(E,T_E)\) acting, and say what distinguishes the Serre–Tate lift.
  5. Explain why \((p)\) is a square-zero ideal in \(\Wt(k)\), and identify the \(\cO_X\)-module \((p)\) (i.e. compute \(p\,\Wt(k)\) as a \(k\)-module via multiplication by \(p\)).
