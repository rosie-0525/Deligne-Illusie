6. The key computation: lift Frobenius, divide by p

Everything in Theorem 2.1 turns on a single calculation, and it is genuinely small. On differentials \(F^{*}=0\) (Lesson 2): the pullback of forms along relative Frobenius vanishes on the nose. So if \(F\) lifts to a map \(\tF\) over \(\Wt(k)\), the lifted pullback \(\tF^{*}\) is not zero but is divisible by \(p\) — it carries the obstruction "\(F^{*}\) ought to be \(\tfrac1p\) of something." Divide it by \(p\). The resulting map \(f=\tfrac1p\tF^{*}\) is closed, hence a morphism of complexes, and on cohomology it is exactly the Cartier isomorphism (Lesson 3). Dividing a lift of Frobenius by \(p\) literally manufactures a representative of the Cartier class. This lesson does that computation in full, twice over examples, and isolates exactly why the hypothesis must be "lift mod \(p^{2}\)".

Setup and hypothesis

Let \(k\) be a perfect field of characteristic \(p\), \(S=\Spec k\), and \(\tS=\Spec\Wt(k)\). Let \(X\) be smooth over \(k\) with a smooth lift \(\tX\) over \(\Wt(k)\). Let \(\sigma\colon\Wt(k)\to\Wt(k)\) be the Frobenius lift on the Witt vectors and form the twisted lift \(\tX' := \tX\times_{\sigma}\Wt(k)\); it reduces mod \(p\) to the Frobenius twist \(X'\). Throughout this lesson we make the optimistic hypothesis that the relative Frobenius \(F\colon X\to X'\) lifts to a \(\Wt\)-morphism \[ \tF\colon \tX\longrightarrow \tX', \qquad \tF \text{ covers } F . \] Globally such an \(\tF\) need not exist — assembling local lifts is the work of Lesson 8. But \(\tF\) does exist locally on affine opens (lift the coordinates), and that is all the local computation needs.

The division

Construction (2.1(b))

The lift \(\tF^{*}\colon\Om^1_{\tX'/\Wt}\to\tF_*\Om^1_{\tX/\Wt}\) has image inside \(p\,\tF_*\Om^1_{\tX}\). Hence there is a unique \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear map \[ f := p^{-1}\,\tF^{*}\;\colon\; \Om^1_{X'/k}\longrightarrow F_*\Om^1_{X/k} \] fitting the square below, in which the right vertical "\(\cdot p\)" is an isomorphism.

\[ \xymatrix@C=3.4pc@R=2.4pc{ \Om^1_{X'/k} \ar[r]^-{\tF^{*}} \ar@{=}[d] & p\,\tF_*\Om^1_{\tX} \\ \Om^1_{X'/k} \ar[r]^-{f} & F_*\Om^1_{X/k} \ar[u]_-{\cdot p}^-{\sim} } \]
Proof

Divisibility. The reduction of \(\tF^{*}\) mod \(p\) is \(F^{*}\), which is \(0\) on \(\Om^1\) (Lemma 2.2). Therefore the image of \(\tF^{*}\) lies in \(p\,\tF_*\Om^1_{\tX}\); we verify this on generators in (1)–(2) below.

The "\(\cdot p\)" isomorphism. Because \(\tX\) is flat over \(\Wt(k)\), multiplication by \(p\) sits in a short exact sequence \(0\to\cO_X\xto{\;\cdot p\;}\cO_{\tX}\to\cO_X\to 0\), so \(\cdot p\colon \cO_X=\cO_{\tX}/p\iso p\,\cO_{\tX}\) is an isomorphism onto the subsheaf \(p\,\cO_{\tX}\). Since \(\Om^1_{\tX/\Wt}\) is locally free and \(\Om^1_{\tX/\Wt}\otimes\cO_X=\Om^1_{X/k}\), the same holds for forms: \(\cdot p\colon F_*\Om^1_{X/k}\iso p\,\tF_*\Om^1_{\tX}\). Composing \(\tF^{*}\) with the inverse of this isomorphism defines \(f\), and uniqueness is forced by the square. ∎

The local formulas

Work on an affine open and let \(x\) be a local section of \(\cO_{\tX}\) with reduction \(x_0\) mod \(p\). The map \(\tF\) lifts \(F\), and \(F^{*}(x_0\otimes 1)=x_0^{p}\), so any lift has the shape

\[ \tag{1} \tF^{*}(x\otimes 1) \;=\; x^{p} + p\,u(x), \qquad u(x)\in\cO_{\tX}. \]

The function \(u(x)\) records "which" lift of \(x\mapsto x^{p}\) we chose; it is the only freedom in \(\tF\). Now apply the (W\(_2\)-linear) de Rham differential \(d\) and use that \(\tF^{*}\) commutes with \(d\): \[ \tF^{*}\!\bigl(d(x\otimes 1)\bigr)=d\,\tF^{*}(x\otimes 1)=d\bigl(x^{p}+p\,u(x)\bigr) = p\,x^{p-1}\,dx + p\,d u(x) \;\in\; p\,\tF_*\Om^1_{\tX}. \] This both confirms the divisibility and lets us divide by \(p\). By definition \(\tF^{*}=(\cdot p)\circ f\) on \(d(x\otimes1)\), so \(p\cdot f(dx_0\otimes1)=p\,(x_0^{p-1}dx_0+du)\); cancelling the (injective) \(\cdot p\) and reducing mod \(p\),

\[ \tag{2} f(dx_0\otimes 1) \;=\; x_0^{\,p-1}\,dx_0 + d\,u(x), \]

where on the right everything is read in \(\cO_X\), \(\Om^1_{X/k}\). This is the master formula. Its first consequence:

\[ \tag{3} d\circ f = 0 . \]

Indeed \(d\bigl(x_0^{p-1}dx_0+du\bigr)=d\bigl(x_0^{p-1}\bigr)\wedge dx_0 + d(du) =(p-1)\,x_0^{p-2}\,dx_0\wedge dx_0 = 0\), the first term because \(dx_0\wedge dx_0=0\) and the second because \(d\circ d=0\).

What (3) buys us

By (3), \(f\) lands in the closed \(1\)-forms \(Z^{1}=\ker\!\bigl(d\colon F_*\Om^1_{X/k}\to F_*\Om^2_{X/k}\bigr)\). Placing \(\Om^1_{X'/k}\) in degree \(1\), this says exactly that \[ f\;\colon\; \Om^1_{X'/k}[-1]\longrightarrow F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k} \] is a morphism of complexes of \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules — a closed form in degree \(1\), zero elsewhere, automatically commutes with the differentials.

The miracle: \(\mathscr{H}^1 f = C^{-1}\)

The Cartier isomorphism \(C^{-1}\colon\Om^1_{X'/k}\iso\mathscr{H}^1\!\bigl(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\bigr)\) is pinned down (Lesson 3) by \(C^{-1}(dx_0\otimes1)=\bigl[x_0^{p-1}dx_0\bigr]\), the class of \(x_0^{p-1}dx_0\) in \(\mathscr{H}^1=Z^1/B^1\). Read (2) modulo exact forms: \(d u(x)\in B^1\) is a coboundary, so

\[ \bigl[f(dx_0\otimes1)\bigr]=\bigl[x_0^{p-1}dx_0 + du\bigr]=\bigl[x_0^{p-1}dx_0\bigr] = C^{-1}(dx_0\otimes 1). \]

Hence \(\mathscr{H}^1 f = C^{-1}\). The Cartier class is what you get by dividing a lift of Frobenius by \(p\). Note the local choice \(u\) — i.e. the choice of \(\tF\) — visibly drops out on cohomology: it only shifts \(f\) by the exact form \(du\). Remember that; it is the seed of the homotopies in Lesson 8.

Worked example — the affine line, in full

Take \(X=\Aa^1=\Spec k[t]\), \(\tX=\Spec\Wt[t]\), \(\tX'=\Aa^1_{\Wt}=\Spec\Wt[t']\), and the simplest lift \(\tF\colon t'\mapsto t^{p}\) (so \(u=0\)). Then \[ f(dt') = p^{-1}\,\tF^{*}(dt') = p^{-1}\,d(t^{p}) = p^{-1}\bigl(p\,t^{p-1}\,dt\bigr) = t^{p-1}\,dt . \] This is closed (\(d(t^{p-1}dt)=(p-1)t^{p-2}\,dt\wedge dt=0\)) and is precisely \(C^{-1}(dt')\), the generator of \(\mathscr{H}^1\). The two pieces of the eventual decomposition (Lesson 7 builds the rest) are visible here: \[ \varphi^{0}\colon\cO_{X'}\to F_*\cO_X,\ \ t'\mapsto t^{p};\qquad \varphi^{1}\colon \Om^1_{X'}\to Z^1,\ \ dt'\mapsto t^{p-1}dt . \]

Worked example — a twisted lift (dependence on \(\tF\))

Keep \(X=\Aa^1\) but choose \(\tF\colon t'\mapsto t^{p}+p\,g(t)\) for a polynomial \(g\) (lifted to \(\Wt[t]\)). Now \(u=g\), and \[ f(dt') = p^{-1}\,d\bigl(t^{p}+p\,g\bigr)= p^{-1}\bigl(p\,t^{p-1}dt + p\,g'\,dt\bigr) = t^{p-1}\,dt + g'(t)\,dt = t^{p-1}dt + dg . \] So \(f\) changed: it picked up \(dg\). But its class did not, since \(dg=g'\,dt\) is exact: \(\bigl[t^{p-1}dt+dg\bigr]=\bigl[t^{p-1}dt\bigr]=C^{-1}(dt')\). Conclusion. Two lifts of \(F\) give maps \(f_1,f_2\) differing by an exact form — a chain homotopy — hence the same map on \(\mathscr{H}^{\bullet}\). The cochain \(f\) depends on \(\tF\); the cohomology class does not.

All degrees at once (Remark 2.2(ii))

Suppose now \(\tF\) exists globally (e.g. for nice \(X\)). Then \(f\) propagates to every degree. Define, for each \(i\geqslant 0\), \[ \varphi^{i}_{(\tX,\tF)} \;=\; \bigl(\Lambda^{i}\Om^1_{X'}\xrightarrow{\ \Lambda^{i}f\ } \Lambda^{i}Z^1 \xrightarrow{\ \wedge\ } Z^{i}\subset F_*\Om^i_{X/k}\bigr), \] i.e. wedge together \(i\) copies of \(f\) and multiply. Because a wedge of closed forms is closed, this lands in \(Z^{i}\); assembling the degrees gives a morphism of complexes \[ \varphi_{(\tX,\tF)} \;\colon\; \bigoplus_{i\geqslant 0}\Om^i_{X'/k}[-i]\longrightarrow F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k} \] which induces \(C^{-1}\) on every \(\mathscr{H}^i\) (the Cartier isomorphism is multiplicative, so \(\mathscr{H}^i(\varphi)=\Lambda^i C^{-1}=C^{-1}\)). It is therefore a quasi-isomorphism. Its truncation \(\tau_{<p}\,\varphi_{(\tX,\tF)}\) is the map \(\varphi_{\tX}\) of Theorem 2.1. The multiplicative bookkeeping — and the reason the range \(i<p\) appears — is the content of Lesson 7.

Why \(\Wt\), and exactly mod \(p^{2}\)

The construction divides by \(p\) once. To do so legally we need \(\tF\) and the equation \(\tF^{*}(x\otimes1)=x^{p}+p\,u\) defined modulo \(p^{2}\): the term \(p\,u\) must be a real, nonzero datum, which it is exactly in \(\Wt=\WW/p^{2}\). Modulo \(p\) alone, \(F^{*}=0\) carries no information; working over \(W_3\) or all of \(\WW(k)\) supplies more than the one division uses. This is the precise reason the hypothesis of the theorem is "lift mod \(p^{2}\)" and nothing more.

Local datum, global obstruction

The map \(f\) is canonical given the pair \((\tX,\tF)\). But \(\tF\) is only a local object: in general it exists on affines, not globally. As the twisted-lift example showed, two local \(\tF\)'s give \(f\)'s differing by an explicit homotopy. So globally \(f\) is well defined only up to homotopy, which forces the whole construction into the derived category \(D(X')\) and demands a gluing argument — precisely Lesson 8.

"Because of a \(1/p\)" — the tie to Lesson 3

In Lesson 3 the class \(x^{p-1}dx\) is nonexact "because of a hidden \(1/p\)": morally it is \(\tfrac1p\,d(x^{p})\), but \(\tfrac1p\) is illegal in characteristic \(p\). The lift mod \(p^{2}\) is exactly what makes \(\tfrac1p\,d(\tF^{*}\!-)\) a legal operation, and \(f=p^{-1}\tF^{*}\) supplies that division. The Cartier class is the obstruction to dividing by \(p\); a lift mod \(p^{2}\) removes it.

Remark 2.2(i): the field need not be perfect

Perfectness of \(k\) is convenient but not essential to the computation. It suffices that \(S\) is the reduction mod \(p\) of a \(\Z/p^{2}\)-flat base \(\tS\) carrying a Frobenius lift \(F_{\tS}\); one then sets \(\tX':=\tX\times_{F_{\tS}}\tS\) and runs the identical argument. This is the door to the relative and logarithmic versions in Lesson 13.

Exercises
  1. Carry out the \(\Aa^1\) computation above from scratch: write \(\tX,\tX',\tF\), compute \(f(dt')\), and check directly that it is closed and generates \(\mathscr{H}^1\).
  2. For \(\Gm=\Spec k[t,t^{-1}]\) take \(\tF\colon t\mapsto t^{p}\). Using multiplicativity \(\varphi^{0}(t'^{-1})=t^{-p}\), show \(f\bigl(\tfrac{dt'}{t'}\bigr)=\tfrac{dt}{t}\), i.e. \(C^{-1}(d\log t')=[d\log t]\). (Compute \(f(d t'/t')=p^{-1}\tF^{*}(d t'/t')\) and use \(d\log(t^p)=p\,d\log t\).)
  3. Verify (1)–(3) for a general smooth affine \(X\): show every lift of \(x\mapsto x^p\) has the form (1), derive (2) by applying \(d\) and dividing by \(p\), and deduce (3).
  4. Let \(\tF_1,\tF_2\) be two local lifts, with \(\tF_i^{*}(x\otimes1)=x^p+p\,u_i\). Show \(f_1-f_2 = d\,h\) where \(h(x\otimes1)=u_1(x)-u_2(x)\) defines a map \(\Om^1_{X'}\to F_*\cO_X\); conclude \(\mathscr{H}^1 f_1=\mathscr{H}^1 f_2\).
  5. For \(\Aa^2\) with \(\tF\colon(t_1,t_2)\mapsto(t_1^{p},t_2^{p})\), compute \(\Lambda^2 f(dt_1'\wedge dt_2') = (t_1 t_2)^{p-1}\,dt_1\wedge dt_2\); check it is closed and equals \(C^{-1}(dt_1'\wedge dt_2')\).
  6. Pinpoint, line by line in the construction, the unique place where \(\Wt(k)=\WW/p^2\) is used (as opposed to \(W_1=k\), which kills \(F^{*}\), or \(W_3\), which is superfluous).
