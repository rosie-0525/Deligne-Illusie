7. From φ¹ to all φⁱ, and why i < p

Lesson 6 extracted, from a local lift of Frobenius mod \(p^2\), a single morphism in the derived category \[ \varphi^1 = f \;:\; \Om^1_{X'/k}[-1] \longrightarrow F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}, \qquad \cH^1\varphi^1 = C^{-1}. \] It splits off the degree-\(1\) cohomology of the pushed-forward de Rham complex. To prove Theorem 2.1 we must do the same in every degree below \(p\) and package the results into one isomorphism \[ \varphi_{\tX} \;=\; \sum_{i<p}\varphi^i \;:\; \bigoplus_{i<p}\Om^i_{X'/k}[-i] \;\iso\; \tau_{<p}\,F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}. \] Remarkably, this needs almost no new geometry. Everything above degree \(1\) is multilinear algebra applied to the one map \(\varphi^1\) — and the single step that can fail, dividing by \(i!\), is exactly what pins the range to \(i<p\).

The reduction: an isomorphism in \(D\) is checked on cohomology

The first thing to notice is that the global derived-category statement dissolves into independent problems, one per degree. The reason is structural: a sheaf placed in degree \(i\), written \(L[-i]\), has cohomology concentrated in that one degree, so a direct sum of such has \(\cH^i\bigl(\bigoplus_j L^j[-j]\bigr)=L^i\). A morphism out of it sees, in degree \(i\), only the \(i\)-th summand.

Reduction (paper §2.1(a))

Let \(K\) be a complex of \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules with \(\cH^i K=0\) for \(i\geqslant p\) (e.g. \(K=\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\)). Then to give an isomorphism \(\bigoplus_{i<p}\Om^i_{X'}[-i]\iso K\) in \(D(X')\) is the same as to give, for each \(0\leqslant i<p\), a morphism \(\varphi^i:\Om^i_{X'}[-i]\to K\) whose induced map \(\cH^i\varphi^i:\Om^i_{X'}\to\cH^iK\) is an isomorphism of sheaves.

Indeed, given such \(\varphi^i\), set \(\varphi=\sum_i\varphi^i\). On \(\cH^i\) the summands \(\varphi^j\) with \(j\ne i\) contribute nothing, so \(\cH^i\varphi=\cH^i\varphi^i\), an isomorphism. A morphism of complexes that is an isomorphism on every cohomology sheaf is a quasi-isomorphism, hence an isomorphism in \(D(X')\) (Lesson 5). Conversely any such isomorphism restricts to the summands. So our entire task reduces to the degreewise demand \[ \cH^i\varphi^i = C^{-1}\qquad(i<p), \] and the Cartier isomorphism (Lesson 3) guarantees that \(C^{-1}\) is an isomorphism. We must build the chain-level maps \(\varphi^i\); Cartier supplies their correctness for free.

The two forced maps \(\varphi^0\) and \(\varphi^1\)

The case \(i=0\) is forced and choice-free. Degree-\(0\) Cartier identifies \(\cH^0F_*\Om^{\bullet}\cong\cO_{X'}\) with the \(p\)-th powers inside \(F_*\cO_X\), so the only candidate is the composite \[ \varphi^0 :\; \cO_{X'}\xto{C^{-1}}\cH^0F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\hookrightarrow F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}, \qquad x'\longmapsto x^p. \] It uses no lift at all. The case \(i=1\) is the work of Lesson 6: it is the one place the lift mod \(p^2\) enters. Everything else, we now build out of \(\varphi^1\).

The multiplicative construction of \(\varphi^i\)

The exterior power is a quotient of the tensor power by the wedge (product) map \(m:(\Om^1_{X'})^{\otimes i}\to\Om^i_{X'}\), \(\omega_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega_i\mapsto \omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_i\). When \(i!\) is invertible this surjection has a canonical antisymmetrization section \[ a(\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_i) =\frac{1}{i!}\sum_{s\in\mathfrak{S}_i}\sgn(s)\, \omega_{s(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega_{s(i)}, \qquad m\circ a=\mathrm{id}. \] With \(a\) in hand, define \(\varphi^i\) for \(1\leqslant i<p\) as the composite \[ \varphi^i:\;\Om^i_{X'}[-i]\xto{a[-i]}(\Om^1_{X'})^{\otimes i}[-i] \xto{(\varphi^1)^{\otimes i}}(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k})^{\otimes i} \xto{\ \wedge\ }F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}. \] Read left to right: antisymmetrize a form into a tensor of \(1\)-forms; apply \(\varphi^1\) in each slot (note \((\Om^1_{X'}[-1])^{\otimes i}=(\Om^1_{X'})^{\otimes i}[-i]\), which is why the shifts line up); then multiply, using that \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\) is a sheaf of graded-commutative differential algebras under \(\wedge\). The tensor products here are derived, but harmless: all complexes in sight are bounded complexes of locally free \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules, so derived and ordinary tensor agree. The careful bookkeeping of these tensors and products lives in Lesson 8.

The point: why \(i<p\)

The scalar \(1/i!\) is the entire restriction

The section \(a\) — and hence this construction of \(\varphi^i\) — exists exactly when \(i!\) is invertible in \(k\). Since \(\operatorname{char}k=p\), invertibility means \(p\nmid i!\). The prime \(p\) first divides a factorial at \(i=p\) (its earliest appearance is the factor \(p\) in \(p!\)), so \[ i!\ \text{is invertible in }k\iff p\nmid i!\iff i<p. \] This is the origin of the truncation \(\tau_{<p}\) and of the range \(i<p\) everywhere in the paper. At \(i=p\) we have \(p!\equiv 0\pmod p\), the symbol \(1/p!\) is undefined, and the construction simply breaks. Degrees \(\geqslant p\) are not a matter of working harder along these lines: they require genuinely different tools — Serre/Grothendieck duality when \(\dim\leqslant p\) (Lesson 9) — and beyond that the statement can fail (Lesson 14).

Why \(\cH^i\varphi^i = C^{-1}\)

The verification is a one-line cancellation, and it is the place where multiplicativity of \(C^{-1}\) (Lesson 3) does all the work.

Proof that \(\cH^i\varphi^i=C^{-1}\)

Pass to cohomology sheaves through the three arrows. The product map induces, on \(\cH^{\bullet}\), the wedge product on \(\bigoplus_j\cH^jF_*\Om^{\bullet}\) (the cohomology of a DG-algebra is a graded algebra). The middle arrow induces \((\cH^1\varphi^1)^{\otimes i}=(C^{-1})^{\otimes i}\) on the relevant summand. So for \(\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_i\in\Om^i_{X'}\), \[ \cH^i\varphi^i(\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_i) =\frac{1}{i!}\sum_{s\in\mathfrak{S}_i}\sgn(s)\, C^{-1}\omega_{s(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge C^{-1}\omega_{s(i)}. \] Each \(C^{-1}\omega_j\) is a class in \(\cH^1\), i.e. of odd degree, so reordering the wedge of the \(i\) of them by a permutation \(s\) introduces the factor \(\sgn(s)\). Hence every summand equals \(\sgn(s)^2\,C^{-1}\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge C^{-1}\omega_i=C^{-1}\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge C^{-1}\omega_i\). There are \(i!\) of them, the prefactor \(1/i!\) cancels, and by multiplicativity of \(C^{-1}\), \[ \cH^i\varphi^i(\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_i) =C^{-1}\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge C^{-1}\omega_i =C^{-1}(\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_i). \] Thus \(\cH^i\varphi^i=C^{-1}\), an isomorphism. ∎

By the Reduction, \(\varphi_{\tX}=\sum_{i<p}\varphi^i\) is then an isomorphism on every cohomology sheaf, hence a quasi-isomorphism onto \(\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\), i.e. an isomorphism in \(D(X')\). That is Theorem 2.1 — modulo the one remaining issue, that \(\varphi^1\) was constructed only locally and must be glued (Lesson 8).

Examples

Example — degree \(2\), the cleanest case

For \(\alpha,\beta\in\Om^1_{X'}\), antisymmetrization gives \(a(\alpha\wedge\beta)=\tfrac12(\alpha\otimes\beta-\beta\otimes\alpha)\). Applying \((\varphi^1)^{\otimes 2}\) and then the product, \[ \varphi^2(\alpha\wedge\beta) =\tfrac12\bigl(\varphi^1\alpha\wedge\varphi^1\beta-\varphi^1\beta\wedge\varphi^1\alpha\bigr) =\tfrac12\bigl(\varphi^1\alpha\wedge\varphi^1\beta+\varphi^1\alpha\wedge\varphi^1\beta\bigr) =\varphi^1\alpha\wedge\varphi^1\beta, \] using anticommutativity of \(\wedge\) on \(1\)-forms in \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}\). So \(\varphi^2\) is simply \(\alpha\wedge\beta\mapsto\varphi^1\alpha\wedge\varphi^1\beta\) — but the derivation needed \(2\) invertible, i.e. \(p>2\). For \(p=2\) the range is just \(i\in\{0,1\}\), so \(\varphi^2\) is never formed, consistently.

Example — \(\varphi^2\) explicitly on \(\Aa^2\)

Take \(X=\Aa^2=\Spec k[t_1,t_2]\) with the Frobenius lift \(\tF:(t_1,t_2)\mapsto(t_1^p,t_2^p)\), so by Lesson 6 we have \(\varphi^1(dt_i')=t_i^{p-1}\,dt_i\). Then \[ \varphi^2(dt_1'\wedge dt_2')=\varphi^1(dt_1')\wedge\varphi^1(dt_2') =t_1^{p-1}t_2^{p-1}\,dt_1\wedge dt_2. \] This is a top form on the surface, hence automatically closed (\(\Om^3=0\)), and its class in \(\cH^2\) is \([t_1^{p-1}t_2^{p-1}\,dt_1\wedge dt_2]\). By the multiplicative computation of Cartier on \(\Aa^2\) (Lesson 3), this is precisely \(C^{-1}(dt_1'\wedge dt_2')\). The formula \(\cH^2\varphi^2=C^{-1}\) is visible by hand.

Subtleties to carry forward

Only \(\tau_{<p}\) — the top degrees are recovered separately

The construction produces an isomorphism onto the truncation \(\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) and nothing more. When \(\dim X\leqslant p\) the missing top degrees are pinned down by Serre/Grothendieck duality (Lesson 9), which is what lets degeneration go through in that range. For \(\dim X>p\) the analogous decomposability is genuinely open / can fail (Lesson 14).

When \(\Om^1\) is free: full decomposability (Remark 2.6(iv))

The only obstruction beyond degree \(p\) was the section \(a\) of \(\bigotimes^n\Om^1\to\Om^n\). If such a section happens to exist for every \(n\) by other means — for instance when \(\Om^1_X\) is free, so \(X\) is parallelizable — then the same multiplicative recipe builds \(\varphi^n\) in all degrees, with no \(i<p\) restriction, given only a lift mod \(p^2\). This is Raynaud's observation: an abelian variety has trivial cotangent bundle, so its de Rham complex decomposes fully whenever it lifts mod \(p^2\), regardless of dimension versus \(p\).

All the geometry is in \(\varphi^1\)

Worth saying plainly: \(\varphi^0\) is forced, \(\varphi^1\) carries the lift mod \(p^2\) and the entire geometric content, and every \(\varphi^i\) for \(i\geqslant 2\) is assembled from \(\varphi^1\) by pure multilinear algebra that only ever needs \(i!\) invertible. The deep input enters once; the rest is bookkeeping.

Exercises
  1. Prove \(p\mid i!\iff i\geqslant p\) (use that \(i!\) is a product of integers \(\leqslant i\), and that \(p\) is prime), and conclude that \(1/i!\) exists in a field of characteristic \(p\) iff \(i<p\).
  2. Verify that the antisymmetrization \(a\) is a section of the wedge map \(m:(\Om^1_{X'})^{\otimes i}\to\Om^i_{X'}\), i.e. \(m\circ a=\mathrm{id}\), whenever \(i!\) is invertible. (Apply \(m\) to each term; every permutation contributes \(\sgn(s)^2\) copies of \(\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_i\).)
  3. Re-derive \(\varphi^2(\alpha\wedge\beta)=\varphi^1\alpha\wedge\varphi^1\beta\) from the definition, and find the degree-\(3\) analogue \(\varphi^3(\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\gamma)\) (you should again get \(\varphi^1\alpha\wedge\varphi^1\beta\wedge\varphi^1\gamma\), valid for \(p>3\)).
  4. Carry out the \(\Aa^2\) computation of \(\varphi^2\) above and check directly that its \(\cH^2\)-class equals \(C^{-1}(dt_1'\wedge dt_2')\), using the Cartier basis classes from Lesson 3.
  5. Explain why for \(p=2\) the construction stops at \(i=1\), so it gives only \(\tau_{<2}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\). Which theorem (Lesson 9) is then needed to obtain degeneration for a smooth proper surface in characteristic \(2\)?
  6. For an abelian variety \(A\), pick a basis \(\omega_1,\dots,\omega_g\) of the invariant \(1\)-forms and write down an explicit section \(\bigotimes^n\Om^1_A\to\Om^n_A\) for every \(n\). Deduce, following Remark 2.6(iv), that \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{A/k}\) is fully decomposable once \(A\) lifts mod \(p^2\), with no constraint relating \(g\) and \(p\).
