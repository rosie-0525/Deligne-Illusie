8. Globalizing: homotopies and Čech gluing

In Lesson 6 we turned a lift \(\tF\) of the relative Frobenius into a map \(f=p^{-1}\tF^{*}:\Om^1_{X'/k}[-1]\to F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\) realizing the Cartier isomorphism on \(\cH^1\). But \(\tF\) exists only locally: smoothness gives a lift on each affine of a cover, and the global ones generally do not exist. So we have one map \(f_i\) per open, and they disagree on overlaps. This lesson does the bookkeeping that assembles the \(f_i\) into a single morphism \(\varphi^1_{\tX}:\Om^1_{X'}[-1]\to F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\) in the derived category \(D(X')\). The two ideas: (c) two lifts differ by an explicit homotopy, so the \(f_i\) are coherently homotopic; and (d) "coherently homotopic local maps" is exactly a cocycle in a Čech double complex, which the augmentation quasi-isomorphism converts into a map in \(D(X')\). The leftover — whether the gluing can be undone, i.e. whether \(\tF\) is global — is a cohomology class \(c\), the subject of the closing sections.

(c) Two lifts differ by a homotopy

Work locally, where a common lift \(\tX\) of \(X\) and two lifts \(\tF_1,\tF_2:\tX\to\tX'\) of \(F\) are available. Each \(\tF_i^{*}\) is a ring homomorphism \(\cO_{\tX'}\to\tF_{i*}\cO_{\tX}\) reducing to the same map \(F^{*}\) modulo \(p\). Hence the difference \[ D:=\tF_2^{*}-\tF_1^{*}\;:\;\cO_{\tX'}\longrightarrow p\,\tF_*\cO_{\tX}=p\,F_*\cO_X \] lands in the square-zero ideal \((p)\). Expanding \(D(ab)\) with \(\tF_2^{*}=\tF_1^{*}+D\) and using \(p^2=0\) (so \(D(a)D(b)=0\)) gives \(D(ab)=F^{*}(a)\,D(b)+F^{*}(b)\,D(a)\): \(D\) is an \(F^{*}\)-twisted derivation. Dividing by \(p\) (legal once, exactly as in Lesson 6) yields an honest \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear derivation \(\cO_{X'}\to F_*\cO_X\), i.e. an \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear map

\[ h_{12}\;:\;\Om^1_{X'/k}\longrightarrow F_*\cO_X . \]

Concretely, if \(\tF_i^{*}(x\otimes 1)=x^{p}+p\,u_i(x)\), then \(h_{12}\bigl(d(x\otimes 1)\bigr)=u_2(x)-u_1(x)\). Combining this with formula (2) of Lesson 6, \(f_i\bigl(d(x\otimes1)\bigr)=x^{p-1}dx+d\,u_i(x)\), the \(x^{p-1}dx\) cancels and

\[ \tag{4} f_2-f_1\;=\;d\circ h_{12} \]

as maps \(\Om^1_{X'}\to F_*\Om^1_X\) (here \(d:F_*\cO_X\to F_*\Om^1_X\)). Thus \(f_1\) and \(f_2\), viewed as maps of complexes \(\Om^1_{X'}[-1]\to F_*\Om^{\bullet}_X\), are homotopic, with homotopy \(h_{12}\). This is precisely Lesson 6's observation that changing the lift changes \(f\) by an exact form, now named. For three lifts the \(u_i\) telescope, giving the cocycle relation

\[ \tag{5} h_{12}+h_{23}=h_{13}. \]
Where the homotopy lives

Because \(\Om^1_{X'}\) is locally free, \(h_{12}\in\Hom_{\cO_{X'}}(\Om^1_{X'},F_*\cO_X)= \Theta_{X'/S}\otimes_{\cO_{X'}}F_*\cO_X\), the sheaf of \(F_*\cO_X\)-valued vector fields on \(X'\). This is the same group that controlled the lifts of \(F\) in Lesson 4 — no accident, as (d) will show.

(d) Gluing along a Čech cover

Choose a Zariski cover \(\mathcal{U}=(U_i)\) of \(X\) fine enough that each \(\tU_i'\) carries a lift \(\tF_i:\tU_i\to\tU_i'\). Set \(f_i=p^{-1}\tF_i^{*}\) on \(U_i'\) and, on \(U_{ij}'\), the homotopy \(h_{ij}\) built from \((\tF_i,\tF_j)\). The three relations

\[ \tag{6} d\circ f_i=0,\qquad f_j-f_i=d\circ h_{ij},\qquad h_{ij}+h_{jk}=h_{ik} \]

(the first because \(d(x^{p-1}dx)=0\), the second is (4), the third is (5)) say exactly that \((h_{ij},f_i)\) is a \(1\)-cocycle in the total complex of the Čech double complex \(\cC(\mathcal{U},F_*\Om^{\bullet}_X)\). Recall that complex: with \(\cC^{b}(\mathcal{U},\cF)=\prod_{i_0<\cdots<i_b}\cF(U_{i_0\cdots i_b})\),

\[ \cC(\mathcal{U},\Om^{\bullet})^{n}=\bigoplus_{a+b=n}\cC^{b}(\mathcal{U},\Om^{a}), \qquad d_{\mathrm{tot}}=d_1\pm d_2, \]

where \(d_1\) is the de Rham \(d\) and \(d_2=\delta\) is the Čech differential. The augmentation \(\Om^{\bullet}\to\cC(\mathcal{U},\Om^{\bullet})\) is a quasi-isomorphism (the sheafified Čech complex is a resolution), hence so is its pushforward \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_X\to F_*\cC(\mathcal{U},\Om^{\bullet}_X)\).

Construction (2.1(d))

Define \(\varphi^1_{(\mathcal{U},(\tF_i))}:\Om^1_{X'}[-1]\to F_*\cC(\mathcal{U},\Om^{\bullet}_X)\) on a local section \(\omega\) of \(\Om^1_{X'}\) (sitting in total degree \(1\)) by \[ \omega\;\longmapsto\;\bigl((h_{ij}\,\omega)_{ij}\,,\,(f_i\,\omega)_i\bigr) \in \cC^1(F_*\cO_X)\oplus\cC^0(F_*\Om^1_X). \] Relations (6) are equivalent to \(d_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ\varphi^1=0\), i.e. to \(\varphi^1\) being a map of complexes. Composing with the inverse in \(D(X')\) of the quasi-isomorphism \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_X\to F_*\cC(\mathcal{U},\Om^{\bullet}_X)\) gives \[ \varphi^1_{\tX}\;:\;\Om^1_{X'/k}[-1]\longrightarrow F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\quad\text{in } D(X'), \qquad \cH^1\varphi^1_{\tX}=C^{-1}. \]

The three components of \(d_{\mathrm{tot}}\varphi^1\) in total degree \(2\) are, term by term, the three relations of (6): \(\cC^0(\Om^2)\) gives \(d f_i=0\), \(\cC^1(\Om^1)\) gives \(f_j-f_i=dh_{ij}\), and \(\cC^2(\Om^0)\) gives the cocycle relation (Exercise 2). On \(\cH^1\), \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) is computed by any single \(f_i\), which is \(C^{-1}\) by Lesson 6. This \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) is the input to the multiplicative machine of Lesson 7: cup-producting it with itself yields \(\varphi^i\) for \(i<p\) and assembles \(\varphi_{\tX}\), completing the proof of Theorem 2.1.

Independence of choices. Given two data \((\mathcal{U},(\tF_i))\) and \((\mathcal{V},(\tF_j'))\), form the disjoint-union cover \(\mathcal{U}\amalg\mathcal{V}\), whose lifts restrict from both. The refinement maps \(\cC(\mathcal{U})\to\cC(\mathcal{U}\amalg\mathcal{V})\leftarrow\cC(\mathcal{V})\) are quasi-isomorphisms compatible with the augmentations and carry the respective \(\varphi^1\)'s to a common one. Inverting the augmentations, all three agree in \(D(X')\). Hence \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) depends only on \(\tX\).

This is why one must work in \(D(X')\)

Globally there is no single chain-level map \(\Om^1_{X'}[-1]\to F_*\Om^{\bullet}_X\): the local \(f_i\) are only homotopic, not equal. The cocycle \((h_{ij},f_i)\) packages a "map valued in the de Rham complex, up to coherent homotopy", and the derived category is the natural home for such an object. The price of inverting the augmentation is that \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) is, in general, a zig-zag (span) rather than an honest morphism of complexes.

When Frobenius lifts globally (Remark 2.2(ii))

If \(F\) lifts globally — a single \(\tF\) on all of \(X\) — take the trivial one-element cover. Then all \(h_{ij}\) vanish, the cocycle is just \((f)\), \(F_*\cC=F_*\Om^{\bullet}\), and \(\varphi^1_{\tX}=f\) is an honest quasi-isomorphism of complexes \(\Om^1_{X'}[-1]\xto{f} F_*\Om^{\bullet}_X\): no gluing, no zig-zag (cf. Lesson 6). Whether this happy situation occurs is measured by a single class.

The obstruction class (Remark 2.2(iii))

The lifts of \(F\) over a fixed \(\tX\) form a torsor under \(\sHom(\Om^1_{X'},F_*\cO_X)=\Theta_{X'/S}\otimes_{\cO_{X'}}F_*\cO_X\): two lifts differ by the derivation \(h_{ij}\) of (c), and any derivation can be added. The torsor has a class

\[ c\;\in\;H^1\bigl(X',\,\Theta_{X'/S}\otimes_{\cO_{X'}}F_*\cO_X\bigr), \]

which is, up to sign, the class of the Čech cocycle \((h_{ij})\) and is the obstruction to a global lift \(\tF\): \(c=0\) iff the \(h_{ij}\) are a coboundary, iff the local lifts can be adjusted to agree, iff \(\tF\) exists globally.

Proposition (2.2(iii))

Let \(\pi:F_*\Om^{\bullet}_X\to F_*\cO_X\) be the projection onto the degree-\(0\) quotient (by the subcomplex \(\Om^{\geqslant 1}\)). Then \[ c\;=\;\pi\circ\varphi^1_{\tX}\;\in\;\Hom_{D(X')}\bigl(\Om^1_{X'}[-1],F_*\cO_X\bigr) =\mathrm{Ext}^1\bigl(\Om^1_{X'},F_*\cO_X\bigr)\supseteq H^1(X',\Theta_{X'/S}\otimes F_*\cO_X). \] Equivalently, \(c\) is the obstruction to representing \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) by a genuine map of complexes: \(c=0\) iff \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) is an honest morphism (then \(\tF\) is global, and we are in the previous section).

In the Čech model this is transparent: \(\pi\) kills the \(\Om^1\)-component \((f_i)\) and keeps the \(\cC^1(F_*\cO_X)\)-component \((h_{ij})\), so \(\pi\circ\varphi^1_{\tX}=[(h_{ij})]=c\). Lesson 12 will show that a coarser obstruction — to lifting \(X'\) itself, not \(F\) — controls decomposability of \(\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) exactly (the gerbe theorem).

Example: \(\Pp^1\) is Frobenius-liftable

Example — the simplest case, \(c=0\) by hand

Cover \(\Pp^1\) by \(U_0=\Spec k[t]\) and \(U_1=\Spec k[s]\), glued by \(s=1/t\) on \(\Gm=U_0\cap U_1\). Lift to \(\Wt(k)\) by the evident \(\Pp^1_{\Wt(k)}\), and lift Frobenius on each chart by \[ \tF_0:\;t\mapsto t^{p},\qquad \tF_1:\;s\mapsto s^{p}. \] On the overlap, with \(t'=t\otimes 1\) and \(s'=1/t'\), \[ \tF_0^{*}(s')=\tF_0^{*}(1/t')=1/t^{p}=s^{p}=\tF_1^{*}(s'), \] so the two lifts agree: \(h_{01}=0\), the cocycle is trivial, and \(c=0\). Hence the \(\tF_i\) glue to a global \(\tF\) on \(\Pp^1_{\Wt(k)}\): \(\Pp^1\) is Frobenius-liftable, no Čech gluing is needed, and \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) is an honest quasi-isomorphism. (The same monomial recipe lifts Frobenius on \(\Pp^n\) and on any toric variety with a chosen \(\Wt(k)\)-model.)

Counterpoint — most curves need the gluing

A general smooth proper curve of genus \(\geqslant 1\) admits no global lift of Frobenius: \(c\neq 0\) in \(H^1(X',\Theta_{X'}\otimes F_*\cO_X)\), which is nonzero for dimension reasons. So even though such a curve always lifts mod \(p^2\) (Lesson 4) and \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) certainly exists in \(D(X')\), it is genuinely a zig-zag, and the Čech gluing of (d) is unavoidable. An ordinary elliptic curve over \(\Fp\) is Frobenius-liftable only for its canonical (Serre–Tate) lift; a generic lift has \(c\neq 0\).

Two obstructions, not one

There is a hierarchy. The class \(c\) here obstructs a global \(\tF\) and is finer than the obstruction to decomposability/liftability of \(X'\) in Lesson 12: a global \(\tF\) forces \(X'\) to be liftable, but a liftable \(X'\) need not admit a global \(\tF\) (curves of genus \(\geqslant 1\) witness this). The main theorem needs only a lift of \(X'\); a global Frobenius lift is a bonus that, when present, upgrades the construction from a span to a chain map.

Exercises
  1. From \(\tF_i^{*}(x\otimes1)=x^{p}+p\,u_i(x)\), derive \(h_{12}(d(x\otimes1))=u_2-u_1\), then prove relations (4) and (5) directly. (Check first that \(D=\tF_2^{*}-\tF_1^{*}\) is an \(F^{*}\)-twisted derivation, using \(p^2=0\).)
  2. Write out \(d_{\mathrm{tot}}=d_1\pm d_2\) on the total degree-\(1\) element \((h_{ij},f_i)\) and check that its three degree-\(2\) components — in \(\cC^0(\Om^2)\), \(\cC^1(\Om^1)\), \(\cC^2(\Om^0)\) — are exactly the three relations of (6). Conclude that \((h_{ij},f_i)\) is a \(1\)-cocycle iff \(\varphi^1\) is a chain map.
  3. Carry out the \(\Pp^1\) computation in full: write the charts, the two lifts, and verify \(\tF_0^{*}=\tF_1^{*}\) on \(\Gm\) so that \(h_{01}=0\) and \(c=0\). Then exhibit the resulting global \(\tF\) on \(\Pp^1_{\Wt(k)}\).
  4. Show the lifts of \(F\) over a fixed \(\tX\) form a torsor under \(\Theta_{X'/S}\otimes F_*\cO_X\), and identify its class with the cocycle \((h_{ij})\). (Use that the difference of two lifts is the derivation of (c), and that any derivation may be added.)
  5. Prove \(c=\pi\circ\varphi^1_{\tX}\) and explain why \(c=0\) is equivalent to \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) being representable by a genuine map of complexes (hence to a global \(\tF\)). Where does \(\pi\) discard the \((f_i)\)?
  6. Relate \(c\) to a first-order deformation: interpret \(\Theta_{X'/S}\otimes F_*\cO_X\) and compare \(c\) with the coarser obstruction of Lesson 12. Give the implication that holds (global \(\tF\Rightarrow X'\) liftable) and a counterexample to its converse.
