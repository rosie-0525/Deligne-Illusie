9. Decomposition and Hodge degeneration

Theorem 2.1 (Lesson 8) splits only the truncation \(\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\) — everything up to degree \(p\). Two natural questions remain. First, can the splitting be pushed all the way to the top, so that the whole complex \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) decomposes? Second, what does decomposition buy us about cohomology? This lesson answers both. When \(\dim X\leqslant p\) a duality argument extends the splitting to a full one (Cor. 2.3); taking hypercohomology then forces the Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence to degenerate at \(E_1\) (Cor. 2.4). In arbitrary dimension one still gets degeneration in low degrees (Cor. 2.5). The boundary case \(\dim X=p\) is where the method is sharpest — and just past it, \(\dim X>p\), is where it stops and an honest open problem begins.

From \(\tau_{<p}\) to a full splitting

Corollary (2.3)

Let \(X\) be smooth over a perfect field \(k\) of characteristic \(p\), with \(\dim X\leqslant p\), and suppose \(X\) (equivalently \(X'\)) lifts over \(\Wt(k)\). Then \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\) is decomposable in \(D(X')\): there is an isomorphism \[ \bigoplus_{i} \Om^i_{X'/k}[-i]\;\iso\; F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k} \] inducing the inverse Cartier isomorphism \(C^{-1}\) on every cohomology sheaf \(\cH^i=\Om^i_{X'/k}\) (now for all \(i\), not only \(i<p\)).

Proof

We may assume \(X\) connected, hence of pure dimension \(d\leqslant p\).

Case \(d<p\). Then \(\Om^i_{X/k}=0\) for \(i>d\), so \(\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\) is concentrated in degrees \([0,d]\subseteq[0,p-1]\). Thus \(\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}=F_*\Om^{\bullet}\), and Theorem 2.1 already is the asserted full splitting. Nothing to do.

Case \(d=p\). Now the complex reaches degree \(p\), exactly the one term \(\tau_{<p}\) drops. We recover it by duality. Since \(X\) is smooth of dimension \(p\), the wedge pairing \[ \wedge:\;\Om^i_{X/k}\times\Om^{p-i}_{X/k}\longrightarrow \Om^p_{X/k}=\omega_X \] is perfect (both sides locally free of complementary rank). The relative Frobenius \(F\) is finite and flat, so each \(F_*\Om^i\) is a locally free \(\cO_{X'}\)-module, and Grothendieck–Serre duality for \(F\) supplies the trace \(\mathrm{Tr}_F:F_*\omega_X\to\omega_{X'}=\Om^p_{X'}\). Composing, \[ (\alpha,\beta)\;\longmapsto\; C(\alpha\wedge\beta),\qquad C:=\mathrm{Tr}_F:F_*\Om^p_{X}\to\Om^p_{X'}, \] is a perfect pairing exhibiting \(F_*\Om^{p-i}\cong\sHom_{\cO_{X'}}(F_*\Om^i,\Om^p_{X'})\). The map \(C\) is nothing but the inverse Cartier isomorphism in top degree: every \(p\)-form is closed (\(\Om^{p+1}=0\)), so \(C\) is defined on all of \(F_*\Om^p\), kills exact forms, and induces \(\cH^p\iso\Om^p_{X'}\) — and this top Cartier operator is precisely the trace of the finite flat \(F\) (Exercise 2).

Because \(C\) kills exact forms, \(C\bigl(d(\alpha\wedge\beta)\bigr)=0\), i.e. \(C(d\alpha\wedge\beta)=\pm\,C(\alpha\wedge d\beta)\): the de Rham differential is, up to sign, its own transpose under the pairing. Hence the dual of the complex \((F_*\Om^{\bullet},d)\) is again \((F_*\Om^{\bullet},d)\) up to shift, and duality carries the decomposable subcomplex \(\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}=\tau_{\leqslant p-1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) (degrees \(0,\dots,p-1\)) to the quotient complex \(\tau_{\geqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) (degrees \(1,\dots,p\)). Therefore \(\tau_{\geqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) is decomposable as well.

Now use the truncation triangle \[ \tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\to F_*\Om^{\bullet}\to \cH^p[-p]\xto{e} \tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}[1]. \] \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) is decomposable iff the connecting class \(e\) vanishes. Splitting the target by Theorem 2.1, \(e\) has components \[ e_i\in\Hom_{D(X')}\bigl(\cH^p[-p],\cH^i[-i+1]\bigr)=\Ext^{\,p-i+1}_{\cO_{X'}}(\cH^p,\cH^i) =H^{p-i+1}\!\bigl(X',\sHom(\cH^p,\cH^i)\bigr), \] the last equality because the \(\cH^i\) are locally free (no local \(\Ext\)).

  • \(1\leqslant i\leqslant p-1\): apply \(\tau_{\geqslant 1}\) to the triangle. Naturality of truncation gives a map of triangles whose top class is \(e\) and whose bottom class is the connecting class of \(\tau_{\geqslant1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\); the vertical map kills only the \(i=0\) summand. Since \(\tau_{\geqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) is decomposable, that bottom class vanishes, forcing \(e_i=0\) for every \(i\geqslant1\).
  • \(i=0\): here \(e_0\in H^{p+1}\bigl(X',\sHom(\cH^p,\cO_{X'})\bigr)\). But \(\dim X'=\dim X=p\), so by Grothendieck vanishing the cohomology of any sheaf vanishes above degree \(p\); thus \(H^{p+1}=0\) and \(e_0=0\).

Hence \(e=0\), \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) decomposes, and the splitting induces \(C^{-1}\) on each \(\cH^i\) since it does so in degrees \(<p\) (Thm. 2.1) and in degree \(p\) (the map \(C\) above). ∎

Why \(\dim=p\) is the exact frontier

The argument used two facts about \(d=p\): top-degree duality (to get \(\tau_{\geqslant1}\) decomposable) and cohomological dimension \(=p\) (to kill \(e_0\in H^{p+1}\)). If \(\dim X>p\) both legs break: the missing terms are no longer dual to known ones, and \(e_0\) lives in \(H^{p+1}\) of a space of dimension \(>p\), where there is no reason to vanish. This is precisely the edge of the method — see the open-problem box below.

Hodge degeneration

Corollary (2.4)

If \(X\) is proper and smooth over \(k\), \(\dim X\leqslant p\), and \(X\) lifts over \(\Wt(k)\), then the Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence \(E_1^{ij}=H^j(X,\Om^i_{X/k})\Rightarrow H^{i+j}_{\dR}(X/k)\) degenerates at \(E_1\).

Proof

By Cor. 2.3 there is an isomorphism \(\bigoplus_i\Om^i_{X'}[-i]\iso F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\) in \(D(X')\). Take hypercohomology over \(X'\). On the right, \(F\) is finite (hence affine), so \(F_*\) is exact and \[ \mathbb{H}^n\bigl(X',F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\bigr)=\mathbb{H}^n\bigl(X,\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\bigr) =H^n_{\dR}(X/k). \] On the left, hypercohomology of a sum of shifted sheaves is just a sum: \[ \mathbb{H}^n\Bigl(X',\bigoplus_i\Om^i_{X'}[-i]\Bigr)=\bigoplus_{i}H^{n-i}\bigl(X',\Om^i_{X'/k}\bigr). \] Finally \(X'=X\times_{k,\,\mathrm{Frob}}k\) is \(X\) with scalars extended along the field automorphism \(a\mapsto a^p\) of the perfect field \(k\); base change along an isomorphism of the base preserves all dimensions of coherent cohomology, so \(\dim_k H^{n-i}(X',\Om^i_{X'})=\dim_k H^{n-i}(X,\Om^i_X)=h^{i,\,n-i}\). Combining, \[ \sum_{i+j=n}h^{ij}=\dim_k H^n_{\dR}(X/k)\qquad\text{for every }n, \] which is the numerical criterion for \(E_1\)-degeneration (Lesson 1). ∎

Partial degeneration in any dimension

Corollary (2.5)

If \(X\) is proper smooth over \(k\) and lifts over \(\Wt(k)\) — of arbitrary dimension — then the Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence satisfies \(E_1^{ij}=E_\infty^{ij}\) for all \(i+j<p\): it degenerates in total degrees below \(p\).

Proof sketch

From the short exact sequence of complexes \(0\to\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\to F_*\Om^{\bullet}\to (F_*\Om^{\bullet}/\tau_{<p})\to0\), the quotient lives in degrees \(\geqslant p\), so it contributes nothing to \(\mathbb{H}^n\) for \(n<p\). Hence the inclusion induces \(\mathbb{H}^n(X',\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet})\iso\mathbb{H}^n(X, \Om^{\bullet})=H^n_{\dR}(X/k)\) for \(n<p\). Apply Theorem 2.1 (the truncation is decomposable) and hypercohomology, exactly as in Cor. 2.4, to get \(\bigoplus_i H^{n-i}(X',\Om^i_{X'})\cong H^n_{\dR}(X/k)\) for \(n<p\); the dimension count \(\sum_{i+j=n}h^{ij}=\dim H^n_{\dR}\) then holds for \(n<p\), which is degeneration in those degrees (Exercise 6). ∎

Examples: keep returning to the simplest cases

Curves — unconditional degeneration

A smooth proper curve has \(\dim=1\leqslant p\) for every prime \(p\geqslant2\), and curves always lift to \(\Wt(k)\) (Lesson 4). So Cor. 2.4 applies with no extra hypothesis: the Hodge spectral sequence of every smooth proper curve degenerates at \(E_1\). Concretely \(\dim H^1_{\dR}=h^{1,0}+h^{0,1}=g+g=2g\). For genus \(1\) the case \(\dim=p=2\) is the duality case of Cor. 2.3; for \(p\geqslant3\) it is the easy \(\dim<p\) case — either way the conclusion is the same and unconditional.

\(\Pp^2\) and quadric surfaces

For \(\Pp^2\) the Hodge numbers are \(h^{ij}=1\) if \(i=j\leqslant2\) and \(0\) otherwise. It lifts (by \(\Pp^2_{\Wt(k)}\)); for \(p\geqslant3\) we are in the case \(\dim=2<p\), while for \(p=2\) we hit the duality case \(\dim=2=p\). Degeneration gives Betti numbers \(\dim H^0_{\dR}=\dim H^2_{\dR}=\dim H^4_{\dR}=1\), odd ones \(0\) — the count \(\sum_{i+j=n}h^{ij}\) reads \(1,0,1,0,1\). The smooth quadric surface \(\Pp^1\times\Pp^1\) has \(h^{00}=h^{22}=1,\;h^{11}=2\), all others \(0\), giving Betti \(1,0,2,0,1\).

Abelian varieties — the parallelizable shortcut, any dimension

Let \(A\) be an abelian variety of dimension \(g\). Here \(\Om^1_A\) is free (translation gives a global frame), so \(\Om^i_A=\textstyle\bigwedge^i\Om^1_A\) is free of rank \(\binom{g}{i}\). Thus \[ h^{ij}=\dim H^j(A,\Om^i_A)=\binom{g}{i}\dim H^j(A,\cO_A)=\binom{g}{i}\binom{g}{j}, \] using \(H^j(A,\cO_A)=\bigwedge^j H^1(A,\cO_A)\) with \(\dim H^1(A,\cO_A)=g\). By Vandermonde, \(\sum_{i+j=n}\binom{g}{i}\binom{g}{j}=\binom{2g}{n}=\dim H^n_{\dR}(A)\). Note this needs no \(\dim\leqslant p\): because \(\Om^1\) is free, Remark 2.6(iv) (Lesson 7) gives a full decomposition for any \(g\) from a single lift mod \(p^2\) — the parallelizable shortcut, not Cor. 2.3.

K3 surfaces

A K3 surface has \(\dim=2\) and lifts to \(\Wt(k)\). Its Hodge diamond is \(1;\,0,0;\,1,20,1;\,0,0;\,1\), so degeneration gives Betti numbers \(1,0,22,0,1\) — the famous \(b_2=22\). For \(p\geqslant3\) this is the case \(\dim<p\); for \(p=2\) it is the duality case.

What degeneration is — and is not

\(\dim>p\) is open

For proper smooth liftable \(X\) of dimension \(\geqslant p+1\), Deligne–Illusie have neither a proof nor a counterexample that \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) decomposes, or that the spectral sequence degenerates (Remark 2.6(iii)). The dimension count above is genuinely a result about a window \([0,p)\), and the case \(\dim=p\) is its sharp boundary. An honest frontier; see Lesson 14.

Degeneration \(\not\Rightarrow\) Hodge symmetry

Over \(\C\), \(E_1\)-degeneration comes packaged with \(h^{ij}=h^{ji}\). In characteristic \(p\) the symmetry can fail even when degeneration holds (Remark 2.6(ii)): there exist surfaces with \(h^{1,0}\neq h^{0,1}\). So Cor. 2.4 is strictly weaker than the complex Hodge decomposition — it asserts the numbers add up, not that they are symmetric.

Liftability is sufficient, not necessary

Cor. 2.4 gives degeneration from a lift mod \(p^2\); the converse is false. Raynaud produced surfaces that do not lift to \(\Wt(k)\) yet whose Hodge spectral sequence still degenerates, and Mumford produced surfaces whose spectral sequence does not degenerate (so, by the theorem, they cannot lift). Liftability is one sufficient mechanism among others; see Lesson 14.

Exercises
  1. Compute \(H^*_{\dR}(\Pp^n/k)\) from \(h^{ij}=\delta_{ij}\) (\(i\leqslant n\)) and verify \(\sum_{i+j=m}h^{ij}=\dim H^m_{\dR}\): the Betti numbers are \(1,0,1,0,\dots,1\) up to degree \(2n\). (\(\Pp^n\) lifts and is liftable for the relevant primes.)
  2. Check that \((\alpha,\beta)\mapsto C(\alpha\wedge\beta)\) is a perfect \(\cO_{X'}\)-bilinear pairing \(F_*\Om^i\times F_*\Om^{p-i}\to\Om^p_{X'}\), and that the top-degree Cartier operator \(C\) coincides with the trace \(\mathrm{Tr}_F\) of the finite flat \(F\). (Reduce to \(X=\mathbb{A}^p\) with coordinates and compute both sides on monomial forms.)
  3. Pinpoint where \(\dim X=p\) is used in the proof of Cor. 2.3: show that the only place is the vanishing \(H^{p+1}(X',\sHom(\cH^p,\cO_{X'}))=0\), and that this is exactly Grothendieck vanishing above the dimension.
  4. For an abelian variety, derive \(h^{ij}=\binom{g}{i}\binom{g}{j}\) and \(\sum_{i+j=n}h^{ij}=\binom{2g}{n}\). Confirm the answer equals the Betti numbers of the topological torus \((S^1)^{2g}\).
  5. For \(\Pp^1\times\Pp^1\), draw the \(E_1\)-page and identify which differentials could be nonzero. Conclude that, because the Hodge groups sit on the diagonal \(i=j\) while every \(d_r\) moves \((i,j)\mapsto(i+r,j-r+1)\), all differentials land in zero groups — degeneration here is automatic and needs no lifting hypothesis. Contrast with a surface having \(h^{1,0}\neq0\) (e.g. an abelian surface), where \(d_1:H^0(\cO)\to H^0(\Om^1)\) and \(d_1:H^1(\cO)\to H^1(\Om^1)\) are the potentially nonzero maps that degeneration actually kills.
  6. Prove the reduction in Cor. 2.5: using the truncation short exact sequence, show \(\mathbb{H}^n(X',\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet})\cong H^n_{\dR}(X/k)\) for \(n<p\), and deduce \(E_1^{ij}=E_\infty^{ij}\) for \(i+j<p\).
