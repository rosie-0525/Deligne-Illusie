11. Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano vanishing

Kodaira vanishing — an ample line bundle kills the lower cohomology of the canonical sheaf — is, classically, a statement about curvature and harmonic forms on a Kähler manifold. Here we prove it with no metric anywhere in sight. The engine is again characteristic \(p\): once the truncated de Rham complex \(\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) decomposes (the gift of a lift mod \(p^2\), Lesson 9 / Theorem 2.1), a single lemma lets us push a vanishing statement from the \(p\)-th power of a line bundle down to the bundle itself. Iterating this Frobenius descent against a Serre-vanishing seed gives Raynaud's theorem in char \(p\); a reduction-mod-\(p\) argument then yields the classical theorem over \(\C\). Strikingly, ampleness is used only to start the seed — the descent itself is pure homological algebra.

The two theorems

Here is the classical target and its characteristic-\(p\) source. Throughout, \(X\) is projective smooth over a field, of pure dimension \(d\), and \(L\) is a line bundle.

Corollary (2.11) (Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano; Ramanujam)

Let \(K\) have characteristic \(0\) and \(L\) be ample. Then \[ H^j(X,\Om^i\otimes L)=0 \quad\text{for } i+j>d, \] equivalently, by Serre duality, \(H^j(X,\Om^i\otimes L^{-1})=0\) for \(i+j<d\).

Corollary (2.8) (Raynaud)

Let \(k\) have characteristic \(p>0\), let \(X\) be liftable over \(\Wt(k)\), and suppose either (i) \(L\) is ample, or (ii) \(d=2\) and \(L\) is numerically positive. Then \[ \tag{2.8.2} H^j(X,\Om^i\otimes L^{-1})=0 \quad\text{for } i+j<\inf(d,p), \] equivalently, by Serre duality, \[ \tag{2.8.1} H^j(X,\Om^i\otimes L)=0 \quad\text{for } i+j>\sup(d,\,2d-p). \]

Why the range shrinks to \(\inf(d,p)\)

The equivalence \((2.8.1)\Leftrightarrow(2.8.2)\) is Serre duality \(H^j(\Om^i\otimes L^{-1})\cong H^{d-j}(\Om^{d-i}\otimes L)^{\vee}\): the conditions \(i+j<\inf(d,p)\) and \((d-i)+(d-j)>2d-\inf(d,p) =\sup(d,2d-p)\) are the same. The ceiling \(\inf(d,p)\) — not the full \(d\) — is the price of working in char \(p\); see the subtlety box at the end.

The key lemma

Everything sits on one descent step. Let \(M\) be an invertible sheaf on \(X\), and write \(M'\) for its base change to the Frobenius twist \(X'\) (Lesson 2), so that \(F^*M'=M^{\otimes p}\).

Lemma (2.9) (Deligne–Illusie)

Let \(X\) be smooth over \(k\), \(M\) invertible, \(b\) an integer. Suppose (1) \(\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/k}\) is decomposable in \(D(X')\), and (2) \(H^j(X,\Om^i\otimes M^{\otimes p})=0\) for \(i+j<b\). Then \[ H^j(X,\Om^i\otimes M)=0 \quad\text{for } i+j<b. \]

Proof

Projection formula. Since \(F\colon X\to X'\) is finite (hence affine), \(F_*\) is exact and computes cohomology. The \(\Om^i\) are not \(\cO_{X'}\)-linear, but \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) is a complex of \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules (the differential is additive, not \(\cO_X\)-linear — it descends). With \(F^*M'=M^{\otimes p}\) and \(M'\) invertible, the projection formula gives, for every \(i\), \[ H^j(X,\,M^{\otimes p}\otimes\Om^i) \;=\; H^j(X',\,M'\otimes F_*\Om^i). \tag{$\ast$} \]

A hypercohomology spectral sequence. Tensoring the complex of \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) by the invertible \(M'\) is exact, so \(M'\otimes F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) is a well-defined complex with the second spectral sequence of hypercohomology \[ E_1^{ij}=H^j(X',\,M'\otimes F_*\Om^i) \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathbb{H}^{i+j}(X',\,M'\otimes F_*\Om^{\bullet}). \] By \((\ast)\), hypothesis (2) says \(E_1^{ij}=0\) whenever \(i+j<b\); hence \(\mathbb{H}^n(X',M'\otimes F_*\Om^{\bullet})=0\) for \(n<b\).

Truncate, then split. Tensoring the truncation triangle \(\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\to F_*\Om^{\bullet}\to\tau_{\geqslant b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\xto{+1}\) by the flat \(M'\) preserves it, and \(\tau_{\geqslant b}\) lives in degrees \(\geqslant b\), so for \(n<b\) \[ \mathbb{H}^n(X',M'\otimes F_*\Om^{\bullet})=\mathbb{H}^n(X',M'\otimes\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}). \] By hypothesis (1), \(\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\iso\bigoplus_{i<b}\Om^i_{X'/k}[-i]\); tensoring this isomorphism by \(M'\) gives \(M'\otimes\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\iso\bigoplus_{i<b} (M'\otimes\Om^i_{X'})[-i]\). Taking \(\mathbb{H}^n\), \[ 0=\mathbb{H}^n(X',M'\otimes\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{i}H^{\,n-i}(X',M'\otimes\Om^i_{X'/k}) \quad (n<b), \] so each summand vanishes: \(H^{\,n-i}(X',M'\otimes\Om^i_{X'})=0\) for \(n<b\), all \(i\).

Untwist. Over the perfect field \(k\), \(X'\) is the base change of \(X\) along the field automorphism \(\sigma=(\,\cdot\,)^p\) of \(k\), and \(M'\otimes\Om^i_{X'}\) is the base change of \(M\otimes\Om^i_X\). Cohomology base-changes, so \(\dim_k H^{n-i}(X',M'\otimes\Om^i_{X'})= \dim_k H^{n-i}(X,M\otimes\Om^i)\). Hence \(H^{n-i}(X,M\otimes\Om^i)=0\) for \(n<b\), all \(i\); that is, \(H^j(X,\Om^i\otimes M)=0\) for \(i+j<b\). ∎

Frobenius descent (proof of 2.8.2)

This is Szpiro's idea. Take \(M=L^{-1}\), so \(M^{\otimes p}=L^{-p}\), and set \(b=\inf(p,d)\). Because \(b\leqslant p\) and \(X\) lifts over \(\Wt(k)\), hypothesis (1) of Lemma 2.9 holds: \(\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) is decomposable by Theorem 2.1 (Lesson 9). The lemma now reads: if \((2.8.2)\) holds for \(L^{-p}\), it holds for \(L^{-1}\). Applied to \(M=L^{-p^n}\), it says vanishing for \(L^{-p^{n+1}}\) descends to vanishing for \(L^{-p^{n}}\).

The seed. By Serre duality \(H^j(\Om^i\otimes L^{-N})\cong H^{d-j}(\Om^{d-i}\otimes L^{N})^{\vee}\). For \(j<d\) we have \(d-j>0\), and Serre vanishing makes \(H^{>0}(\Om^{d-i}\otimes L^{N})=0\) once \(N\gg0\) (here \(L\) ample is used — and only here). So for \(N\gg0\), \[ H^j(X,\Om^i\otimes L^{-N})=0 \quad\text{for } j<d,\ \text{in particular for } i+j<b. \] Choose \(m\) with \(N=p^{m}\) this large.

Descend. Starting from the seed at \(L^{-p^{m}}\), apply Lemma 2.9 to \(M=L^{-p^{m-1}},L^{-p^{m-2}},\dots,L^{-p^{0}}\) in turn. After \(m\) steps, \[ H^j(X,\Om^i\otimes L^{-1})=0 \quad\text{for } i+j<\inf(p,d), \] which is \((2.8.2)\). For case (ii) the seed instead rests on results of Szpiro guaranteeing \(H^j(\Om^i\otimes L^{-N})=0\) for numerically positive \(L\) on a surface; the descent is identical.

Where the hypotheses actually enter

The proof cleanly separates two roles. Ampleness is a one-time input: it seeds the vanishing at a single huge power \(L^{-p^{m}}\) through Serre vanishing, and is never used again. The lift mod \(p^2\) powers every descent step, through the decomposability of \(\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\). Remove the lift and the chain snaps at the first rung — which is exactly how Kodaira vanishing can fail in char \(p\).

Back to characteristic 0 (proof of 2.11)

The deduction copies the degeneration argument of Lesson 10 verbatim. Spread \((X,L)\) over a finitely generated \(\Z\)-algebra and reduce modulo a prime \(p\geqslant d\). The reduction is projective smooth of dimension \(d\), carries the reduction of \(L\) (still ample), and — being a reduction — automatically lifts mod \(p^2\). Apply \((2.8.2)\): since \(p\geqslant d\) we have \(\inf(d,p)=d\), so \(H^j(\Om^i\otimes L^{-1})=0\) for the full range \(i+j<d\). Cohomology and Hodge numbers are constant in the family for \(p\gg0\), so the vanishing lifts back to the generic, and hence to the complex, fibre. Choosing \(p\geqslant d\) is what buys the full classical range.

A weak Lefschetz theorem

Corollary (2.12) (Berthelot)

Let \(X\) be projective smooth of dimension \(d\) over \(k\), and \(D\subset X\) a smooth ample divisor, with \(X\) and \(D\) both liftable over \(\Wt(k)\). Then the restriction \(H^n_{\dR}(X)\to H^n_{\dR}(D)\) is an isomorphism for \(n<\inf(p,d)-1\) and injective for \(n=\inf(p,d)-1\).

The proof is a weight/log dévissage: the short exact sequence of complexes \[ 0\to\Om^{\bullet}_X(-D)\to\Om^{\bullet}_X(\log D)(-D)\to\Om^{\bullet}_D[-1]\to0 \] reduces the statement to the vanishing of \(\mathbb{H}^*\) of \(\Om^{\bullet}_X(-D)\) in a range, which \((2.8.2)\) supplies (here \(L=\cO_X(D)\)). We defer the logarithmic complex \(\Om^{\bullet}_X(\log D)\) and this dévissage to Lesson 13.

Examples

Example — curves (\(d=1\)), the cleanest check

Here \(\inf(1,p)=1\), so \((2.8.2)\) covers only \(i+j<1\), i.e. \((i,j)=(0,0)\): it asserts \(H^0(X,\Om^0\otimes L^{-1})=H^0(X,L^{-1})=0\) for \(L\) ample. And indeed an ample \(L\) on a connected proper curve has \(\deg L>0\), so \(\deg L^{-1}<0\), so \(L^{-1}\) has no nonzero global sections. KAN on curves is just "a negative-degree line bundle has no sections" — a reassuring base case.

Example — \(\Pp^d\), \(L=\cO(1)\), versus Bott

\(\Pp^d\) is defined over \(\Z\), hence lifts; \(\cO(1)\) is ample. So \((2.8.2)\) gives \(H^j(\Pp^d,\Om^i(-1))=0\) for \(i+j<\inf(d,p)\). The Bott formula computes all \(H^j(\Pp^d,\Om^i(t))\) and confirms each instance. The smallest nontrivial case is \(d=2\), \((i,j)=(1,0)\), \(i+j=1<2\): \(H^0(\Pp^2,\Om^1(-1))=0\), i.e. there is no nonzero \(1\)-form twisted down by \(\cO(1)\) — true by Bott. For \((i,j)=(0,1)\) one gets \(H^1(\Pp^2,\cO(-1))=0\), again standard.

Example — one descent step on \((\Pp^2,\cO(1))\), \(p=2\)

Take \(p=2\), \(d=2\), so \(b=\inf(2,2)=2\) and \(\tau_{<2}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) decomposes. Suppose we already know \((2.8.2)\) for \(L^{-p}=\cO(-2)\): \(H^j(\Pp^2,\Om^i(-2))=0\) for \(i+j<2\). Apply Lemma 2.9 with \(M=\cO(-1)\) (so \(M^{\otimes p}=\cO(-2)\)). Hypothesis (2) is exactly the assumed vanishing for \(\cO(-2)\); hypothesis (1) is the decomposition. The conclusion is \(H^j(\Pp^2,\Om^i(-1))=0\) for \(i+j<2\) — the previous example, manufactured from its own square.

Subtleties

In char \(p\) the range stops at \(p\), not at \(d\)

\((2.8.2)\) only vanishes for \(i+j<\inf(d,p)\). If \(d>p\) this is a genuinely weaker statement than Kodaira vanishing: there can be nonzero \(H^j(\Om^i\otimes L^{-1})\) with \(p\leqslant i+j<d\). The full classical range needs either \(d\leqslant p\) (then \(\inf(d,p)=d\)) or the reduction-mod-\(p\) trick with \(p\gg d\), as in (2.11). This is the same ceiling that limits Theorem 2.1 to \(\tau_{<p}\).

Kodaira vanishing is false in char \(p\) without a lift (Remark 2.10(ii))

Raynaud and Szpiro constructed smooth projective surfaces over a field of characteristic \(p\) carrying an ample \(L\) with \(H^1(X,L^{-1})\neq0\) — a flat contradiction of Kodaira vanishing. By \((2.8)\) such an \(X\) cannot lift to \(\Wt(k)\), and indeed it does not. So Kodaira vanishing is not a theorem in char \(p\) on its own; liftability is doing real work (Lesson 14).

Liftability is sufficient, not necessary (Remark 2.10(iii))

The converse is also subtle: Raynaud exhibited a surface that does not lift mod \(p^2\) yet for which KAN nonetheless holds for every ample \(L\). Liftability guarantees vanishing but is not forced by it — the boundary between the two is the gerbe story (Lesson 12, Lesson 14).

Exercises
  1. Prove the projection-formula identity \(H^j(X,M^{\otimes p}\otimes\Om^i)=H^j(X',M'\otimes F_*\Om^i)\): use \(F^*M'=M^{\otimes p}\), the projection formula \(F_*(F^*M'\otimes\Om^i)\cong M'\otimes F_*\Om^i\), and that \(F\) is affine so \(R^{q}F_*=0\) for \(q>0\).
  2. Deduce \((2.8.1)\Leftrightarrow(2.8.2)\) from Serre duality \(H^j(\Om^i\otimes L^{-1})\cong H^{d-j}(\Om^{d-i}\otimes L)^{\vee}\), checking that \(i+j<\inf(d,p)\) and \((d-i)+(d-j)>\sup(d,2d-p)\) are equivalent.
  3. Prove the curve case directly: an ample \(L\) on a smooth connected proper curve has \(H^0(X,L^{-1})=0\). (Degree of a sub-line-bundle of \(\cO_X\).)
  4. Using the Bott formula, verify \((2.8.2)\) for \(\Pp^3\), \(L=\cO(1)\) in the two cases \((i,j)=(1,0)\) and \((i,j)=(2,0)\) (assume \(p>3\)), and exhibit a pair \((i,j)\) with \(i+j\geqslant\inf(3,p)\) where the relevant group is nonzero.
  5. Carry out the descent step from \(L^{-p^2}\) to \(L^{-p}\): identify the bundle \(M\), check both hypotheses of Lemma 2.9, and state the conclusion.
  6. Explain why the descent yields vanishing only up to \(\inf(d,p)\), tracing the bound to the restriction \(b\leqslant p\) in the decomposability of \(\tau_{<b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) and to the dimension cutoff \(\Om^i=0\) for \(i>d\).
