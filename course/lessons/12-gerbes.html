12. Gerbes: lifting ⇔ splitting, exactly

Lessons 6–8 proved an implication: a lift of \(X'\) one infinitesimal step — from a base \(S\) of characteristic \(p\) to a flat \(\Z/p^2\)-thickening \(\tS\) — produces a splitting of the truncated complex \(\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\). Lesson 5 then noticed something suspicious: the lifts of \(X'\) and the splittings of that complex are governed by the same cohomology — obstruction in \(H^2(X',\Theta_{X'/S})\), choices a torsor under \(H^1(X',\Theta_{X'/S})\). This lesson shows that the coincidence is an honest equivalence. Each problem is classified by a gerbe, and Giraud's machine reads off an \(H^2\)-obstruction and an \(H^1\)-torsor from any gerbe. Theorem 3.5 builds a canonical equivalence between the two gerbes; everything else — same obstruction, same torsor of solutions — is then automatic. As a bonus the argument runs over a general base, so Theorem 2.1 escapes the perfect-field hypothesis.

A two-line gerbe primer

A gerbe is the \(H^2\)-analogue of a torsor. Recall an \(\mathcal{A}\)-torsor (\(\mathcal{A}\) an abelian sheaf on a site \(T\)) is a sheaf of sets that is locally nonempty with a simply transitive \(\mathcal{A}\)-action; its isomorphism class lives in \(H^1(T,\mathcal{A})\), and it has a global section iff that class is \(0\). Replace "sheaf of sets" by "stack of groupoids" and shift degree by one:

Definition (gerbe, §3 / Giraud)

A gerbe on \(T\) bound by an abelian sheaf \(\mathcal{A}\) is a stack of groupoids \(\mathcal{G}\) that is (i) locally nonempty — every point has a neighbourhood over which \(\mathcal{G}\) has an object; (ii) locally connected — any two objects over an open are locally isomorphic; and (iii) equipped with identifications \(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}(\xi)\cong\mathcal{A}\) for every object \(\xi\), compatible with restriction. Its class \(\cl\,\mathcal{G}\in H^2(T,\mathcal{A})\) vanishes iff \(\mathcal{G}\) has a global object; and when it does, the global objects up to isomorphism form a torsor under \(H^1(T,\mathcal{A})\).

The dictionary to keep in view

\(\mathcal{A}\)-torsors \(\leftrightarrow H^1(T,\mathcal{A})\) (a global section is the question); gerbes bound by \(\mathcal{A}\) \(\leftrightarrow H^2(T,\mathcal{A})\) (a global object is the question). The slogan of this lesson: two problems with the same answer are two gerbes with the same class — provided you can write down an equivalence between them. We will.

The two gerbes

Fix a two-term complex \(K\) of \(\cO_T\)-modules with \(K^i=0\) for \(i\neq 0,1\) and \(\cH^1K\) locally free.

Definition (3.2, the gerbe of splittings \(\sc(K)\))

An object over an open \(U\) is a splitting \(s\colon\cH^1K\to K^1\) of the projection \(K^1\twoheadrightarrow\cH^1K\). A morphism \(s'\to s''\) is an \(h\colon\cH^1K\to K^0\) with \(s''=s'+d\circ h\). Composition is addition of \(h\)'s, so \(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}(s)=\{h:dh=0\}=\sHom(\cH^1K,\cH^0K)\). Thus \(\sc(K)\) is a gerbe bound by \(\sHom(\cH^1K,\cH^0K)\), with class \(\cl\,\sc(K)\in H^2\!\bigl(T,\sHom(\cH^1K,\cH^0K)\bigr) =\Ext^2(\cH^1K,\cH^0K)\).

Definition (3.4, the gerbe of liftings \(\Rel(Y,\tS)\))

Let \(Y\) be smooth over \(S\), and \(\tS\) a flat \(\Z/p^2\)-thickening of \(S\). An object over an open \(U\subseteq Y\) is a smooth lift \(\tU/\tS\) reducing to \(U\); a morphism is an \(\tS\)-isomorphism of lifts reducing to the identity on \(U\). Deformation theory gives \(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}(\tU)=\Theta_{Y/S}\) (relative vector fields), so \(\Rel(Y,\tS)\) is a gerbe bound by \(\Theta_{Y/S}\), with class \(\cl\,\Rel(Y,\tS)\in H^2(Y,\Theta_{Y/S})\) — the classical obstruction to a global lift of \(Y\) (Lesson 4).

Splittings vs. decompositions (Proposition 3.3)

First, the splitting gerbe is just the obstruction theory of Lesson 5 in stack form.

Proposition (3.3)

(a) \(\cl\,\sc(K)=-e(K)\), the triangle obstruction of Lesson 5. (b) There is a canonical bijection \[ \alpha\colon \{\text{global objects of }\sc(K)\}/\!\cong\;\iso\;\{\text{decompositions of }K\}, \] equivariant for the action of \(\Ext^1(\cH^1K,\cH^0K)\) on both sides. Hence \(K\) is decomposable \(\iff\sc(K)\) has a global object \(\iff e(K)=0\), and the two solution sets are the same torsor under \(\Ext^1(\cH^1K,\cH^0K)\).

Proof sketch

A global splitting \(s\colon\cH^1K\to K^1\) lands in \(\ker(d\colon K^1\to K^2)=K^1\) and satisfies \(\pi\circ s=\Id\) (\(\pi\) the projection to \(\cH^1K\)); viewing \(\cH^1K[-1]\to K\) it is a chain map inducing \(\Id\) on \(\cH^1\), i.e. a decomposition \(f_1\) (the criterion of Lesson 5). This is \(\alpha\). Two splittings differing by \(d\circ h\) give chain-homotopic \(f_1\)'s, i.e. equal maps in \(D(T)\), so \(\alpha\) is well defined on isomorphism classes and injective; surjectivity is that any \(f_1\) is represented by such an \(s\) since \(\cH^1K\) is locally free. The connecting map of the truncation triangle that defines \(e(K)\) is, term for term, the cocycle that defines \(\cl\,\sc(K)\), with the opposite sign coming from the differential's placement — whence (a). Both torsor structures are by \(\Ext^1(\cH^1K,\cH^0K)\) and \(\alpha\) intertwines them. ∎

The main theorem

Now specialize: \(K=\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\), whose \(K^0=F_*\cO_X\), \(K^1=Z^1\) (closed \(1\)-forms), and whose cohomology, by Cartier (Lesson 3), is \(\cH^0K=\cO_{X'}\), \(\cH^1K=\Om^1_{X'/S}\). The bound of \(\sc(K)\) is then \(\sHom(\Om^1_{X'/S},\cO_{X'})=\Theta_{X'/S}\) — the very bound of \(\Rel(X',\tS)\).

Theorem (3.5)

There is a canonical equivalence of gerbes \[ \Phi\colon \Rel(X',\tS)\;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\;\sc\bigl(\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\bigr) \] inducing the identity on the common bound \(\Theta_{X'/S}\).

Construction of \(\Phi\) (this is Lessons 6 and 8, reread)

Let \(\tU'\) be a local lift of an open \(U'\subseteq X'\). To turn it into a splitting we choose an auxiliary local lift \((\tU,\tF)\) of \((U,F)\) — a lift \(\tU\) of \(U\) and a lift \(\tF\colon\tU\to\tU'\) of relative Frobenius — and set \[ \Phi(\tU')\;:=\;s(\tU,\tF)\;=\;p^{-1}\tF^{*}\colon\Om^1_{X'/S}\longrightarrow Z^1, \] which is exactly the splitting \(f\) of Lesson 6 (divide the lift of Frobenius by \(p\); it is closed and induces \(C^{-1}\) on \(\cH^1\)). The auxiliary choice must drop out: two lifts \(\tF_1,\tF_2\) over the same \(\tU'\) give splittings differing by \(d\circ h_{12}\), where \(h_{12}\colon\Om^1_{X'/S}\to F_*\cO_X\) is the homotopy of Lesson 8; that is precisely a morphism \(s(\tU,\tF_1)\to s(\tU,\tF_2)\) in \(\sc(K)\). The cocycle relation \(h_{12}+h_{23}=h_{13}\) (Lesson 8, eq. (5)) makes these morphisms compose correctly, so \(\Phi\) is a well-defined functor of stacks independent of all auxiliary data. On automorphisms it sends a vector field \(\theta\in\Theta_{X'/S}\) (an infinitesimal automorphism of \(\tU'\)) to the corresponding \(h\), i.e. to \(\theta\) itself: \(\Phi\) is the identity on the bound. A functor of gerbes inducing an isomorphism on bounds is automatically an equivalence. ∎

The punchline (Corollary 3.6)

Corollary (3.6)

(a) \(X'\) admits a global lift over \(\tS\) \(\iff\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is decomposable in \(D(X')\). (b) When this holds, \(\alpha\circ\Phi\) is a bijection \[ \{\text{liftings of }X'\text{ over }\tS\}/\!\cong\;\iso\;\{\text{decompositions of }\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\}, \] equivariant under \(\Ext^1(\Om^1_{X'},\cO_{X'})=H^1(X',\Theta_{X'/S})\). The shared obstruction lives in \(\Ext^2(\Om^1_{X'},\cO_{X'})=H^2(X',\Theta_{X'/S})\).

This is immediate: an equivalence of gerbes equates their classes and their torsors of global objects, then \(\Phi\) carries the lifting problem to the splitting problem and \(\alpha\) carries splittings to decompositions. The map \(\varphi^1_{\tX}=\alpha\circ\Phi(\tX')\) attached to a lift is the one built by hand in Lessons 6 and 8 — now seen to be the canonical content of a gerbe equivalence rather than a string of choices.

The payoff

"Lifting \(X'\) mod \(p^2\)" and "splitting \(\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\)" have the same obstruction (one class in \(H^2(X',\Theta_{X'/S})\)) and the same torsor of solutions (\(H^1(X',\Theta_{X'/S})\)). Lesson 5 could only call this a numerical coincidence; Theorem 3.5 makes it an equivalence of classifying objects. This is, finally, the structural reason a mod-\(p^2\) lift has any right to control de Rham cohomology.

Recovering Theorem 2.1, over any base

Corollary (3.7)

(a) A lift \(\tX'\) of \(X'\) over \(\tS\) yields \(\bigoplus_{i<p}\Om^i_{X'/S}[-i]\iso \tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) inducing \(C^{-1}\) — Theorem 2.1 over a general base. (b) If \(\dim X\leqslant p\), \(X'\) lifts, and \(H^{p+1}(X',\Om^p_{X'})=0\) (e.g. \(S\) affine and \(X\) proper over \(S\)), then \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) is fully decomposable.

For (a), 3.6 splits the degree-\(\leqslant 1\) part; the multiplicative machine of Lesson 7 cup-products \(\varphi^1\) with itself to reach all \(i<p\). For (b), one upgrades from \(\tau_{\leqslant 1}\) to \(\tau_{\leqslant p}\) by the duality argument of Lesson 9, the vanishing \(H^{p+1}(\Om^p_{X'})=0\) killing the last obstruction.

Remarks 3.8 and 3.9

(3.8) The morphism \(\varphi^1_{\tX}=\alpha\circ\Phi(\tX')\) depends only on the lift \(\tX'\) of \(X'\), and two lifts differ exactly by their class in \(H^1(X',\Theta_{X'/S})\) — matching Lesson 6's "a different lift changes \(f\) by a homotopy". (3.9) The gerbe \(\Rel(X',\tS)\) is essentially independent of the thickening: two thickenings \(\tS_1,\tS_2\) give canonically equivalent gerbes (compare lifts through an amalgamated sum \(\tS_1+_S\tS_2\)). So the whole picture depends only on \(X'/S\), not on the chosen \(\Z/p^2\)-extension.

Examples

Affine, and \(\Aa^n\)

If \(X\) is smooth affine then \(H^2(X',\Theta_{X'/S})=0\), so both gerbes have global objects: \(X'\) lifts and \(\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) decomposes. If moreover \(H^1(X',\Theta_{X'/S})=0\) (e.g. \(X=\Aa^n\)), the torsor is a point: a unique lift and a unique decomposition, matching the explicit \(\tF\colon t_i\mapsto t_i^{p}\) computation of Lesson 6.

Smooth proper curves — decompositions are deformations

For \(X/k\) a smooth proper curve, \(H^2(X,\Theta_X)=0\) for dimension reasons, so \(X'\) lifts and \(\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) decomposes. By 3.6(b) the decompositions form a torsor under \(H^1(X,\Theta_X)=H^1(X,T_X)\), the space of first-order deformations, of dimension \(3g-3\) for \(g\geqslant 2\). So: decompositions of the de Rham complex \(\leftrightarrow\) lifts of \(X'\) \(\leftrightarrow\) first-order deformations. The torsor of "ways to split cohomology" is literally the deformation space of the curve.

A hierarchy of obstructions

Lesson 8's class \(c\in H^1(X',\Theta_{X'/S}\otimes F_*\cO_X)\) obstructs a global lift of Frobenius; it is finer than the present obstruction. A global \(\tF\) forces \(X'\) to be liftable (its existence gives a splitting, hence by 3.6 a lift), but not conversely: a curve of genus \(\geqslant 1\) always lifts mod \(p^2\) yet has \(c\neq 0\). The gerbe theorem needs only the coarser datum, a lift of \(X'\).

Subtleties

Only \(\tau_{\leqslant 1}\) is an "iff"

Corollary 3.6 is an equivalence for the truncation in degrees \(\leqslant 1\) only. Decomposing all of \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) needs more — \(\dim\leqslant p\) plus duality (Lesson 9) — and for \(\dim>p\) full decomposition can genuinely fail (Lesson 14).

The pathology, made exact

Specializing 3.6: \(X\) does not lift over \(W_2\) \(\iff\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) is not decomposable in \(D(X')\). Yet this can be invisible to the Hodge spectral sequence and to Kodaira-type vanishing — Raynaud's non-liftable surface (Lesson 14) has degenerate Hodge–de Rham nonetheless. Non-liftability is detected in the derived category even when its cohomological shadows look ordinary. Note also: the obstruction lives on \(X'\), not \(X\) (Lesson 13).

  2. Affine case: deduce from \(H^2=0\) that \(X'\) lifts and \(\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) decomposes, and from \(H^1=0\) (e.g. \(\Aa^n\)) that both are unique.
  3. Curves: for \(g\geqslant 2\), show the torsor of decompositions has dimension \(3g-3\) by identifying it with \(H^1(X,T_X)\). What happens for \(g=0,1\)?
  4. On a toy two-term complex \(K\) (\(K^i=0\), \(i\neq 0,1\), \(\cH^1K\) free) verify directly that \(\cl\,\sc(K)=-e(K)\), tracking the sign through the truncation triangle.
  5. Explain why a global lift of \(F\) (class \(c\), Lesson 8) implies liftability of \(X'\) but is not implied by it; exhibit the genus-\(\geqslant 1\) counterexample.
  6. Deduce 3.7(a) from 3.6 and the multiplicativity of Lesson 7: how does \(\varphi^1_{\tX}\) generate \(\varphi^i\) for \(i<p\)?
