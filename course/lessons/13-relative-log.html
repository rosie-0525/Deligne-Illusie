13. Relative degeneration and logarithmic poles

The theorem we have built — a lift of \(X'\) splits \(\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\), and splitting forces Hodge degeneration — was stated for a single smooth proper variety over a field. Section 4 of the paper pushes it in two directions at once. Relative: replace the base field by an arbitrary scheme \(S\) of characteristic \(p\), so that everything happens in a family \(f:X\to S\); now degeneration must be read off the higher direct images \(R^jf_*\Om^i\), and the prize is not just a numerical identity but the local freeness of those sheaves and their compatibility with base change. Logarithmic: allow \(X\) to carry a normal-crossings divisor \(D\) and work with the de Rham complex with log poles along \(D\), which computes the cohomology of the open complement \(X-D\). The Cartier isomorphism, the splitting, and the degeneration all survive — and as a bonus the log story, reduced mod \(p\), reproves Deligne's classical degeneration for open varieties with no Hodge theory at all. This is Lesson 9 told twice, in families and with poles.

Part A — Relative degeneration (§4.1)

Two spectral sequences over a base (4.1.1)

Let \(S\) be a scheme of characteristic \(p\) and \(f:X\to S\) smooth. The complex \(\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) has two natural filtrations, hence two spectral sequences, both abutting to the relative de Rham cohomology \(R^{*}f_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\). The stupid filtration gives the Hodge spectral sequence \[ \tag{13.1} E_1^{ij}=R^jf_*\Om^i_{X/S}\;\Longrightarrow\;R^{i+j}f_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}. \] The canonical (= "bête by truncation") filtration gives the conjugate spectral sequence \[ \tag{13.2} {}_cE_2^{ij}=R^if'_*\,\cH^j F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\;\Longrightarrow\;R^{i+j}f_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}, \] where \(f':X'\to S\) is the base change of \(f\) along the absolute Frobenius \(F_S\) of \(S\). By the Cartier isomorphism (Lesson 3), \(\cH^j F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\cong\Om^j_{X'/S}\), so the conjugate \(E_2\)-page reads \({}_cE_2^{ij}=R^if'_*\Om^j_{X'/S}\) (cf. Katz). The two spectral sequences carry the same information about the abutment from opposite ends; the conjugate one is the natural home of the decomposition, because it degenerates exactly when \(\tau_{\leqslant b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) decomposes.

Proposition (4.1.2)

Let \(S=\Spec A\) be artinian local with residue field \(k\), and \(f:X\to S\) of finite type; fix \(n\). Suppose (a) the \(H^j(X_s,\Om^i_{X_s/k})\) with \(i+j=n\) are finite-dimensional, and (b) for every closed subscheme \(T\subseteq S\) the conjugate spectral sequence of \(f_T\) degenerates in total degree \(n\) (\({}_cE_2^{ij}={}_cE_\infty^{ij}\) for \(i+j=n\)). Then the Hodge spectral sequence (13.1) satisfies \(E_1^{ij}=E_\infty^{ij}\) for \(i+j=n\), and the \(R^jf_*\Om^i_{X/S}\) with \(i+j=n\) are free \(A\)-modules whose formation commutes with base change.

Proof idea (a length count plus dévissage)

For any coherent term one has \(\lg_A R^jf_*\Om^i_{X/S}\leqslant h^{ij}\cdot\lg(A)\), with equality in degree \(n\) iff that sheaf is free of rank \(h^{ij}\) (here \(h^{ij}=\dim_k H^j(X_s,\Om^i)\)). The abutment \(R^nf_*\Om^{\bullet}\) has a length that both spectral sequences must reproduce. Running \(\sum E_1^{ij}\geqslant \sum E_\infty^{ij}=\lg R^nf_*\Om^{\bullet}\) against the conjugate side, where hypothesis (b) makes \(\sum {}_cE_2^{ij}=\lg R^nf_*\Om^{\bullet}\) on the nose, squeezes every inequality to an equality: the Hodge terms attain their maximal length, so they are free and (13.1) degenerates in degree \(n\). Compatibility with base change is then automatic from freeness. The one real subtlety is that hypothesis (b) must be fed in over every \(T\subseteq S\); one inducts on the nilpotency length of \(A\), using that \(F_S\) factors through \(A/\mathfrak m^{N'}\) for a suitable \(N'\) (Frobenius is highly contracting on a nilpotent ideal), which is what links the conjugate page over \(S\) to those over its closed subschemes. ∎

From decomposability to degeneration in families (4.1.3–4.1.5)

The hypothesis (b) looks unverifiable until one recalls (Lesson 5) that the conjugate degeneration in total degree \(n\) is implied by decomposability of \(\tau_{\leqslant n}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) in \(D(X')\) — and that decomposability is stable under base change, since the cohomology sheaves \(\cH^j=\Om^j_{X'/S}\) are \(S\)-flat (Remark 4.1.3). That stability is exactly what lets one verify (b) for all \(T\subseteq S\) at once, and it is what makes the standard reductions (\(S\) affine, then noetherian, complete, artinian local) legal. Combining with 4.1.2:

Corollary (4.1.4)

Let \(f:X\to S\) be proper and smooth, and suppose \(\tau_{\leqslant b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is decomposable in \(D(X')\). Then for \(i+j\leqslant b\) the sheaves \(R^jf_*\Om^i_{X/S}\) are locally free of finite type, of formation compatible with base change, and the Hodge spectral sequence (13.1) satisfies \(E_1^{ij}=E_\infty^{ij}\) for \(i+j\leqslant b\).

Corollary (4.1.5)

If the full complex \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is decomposable (take \(b\geqslant 2\dim(X/S)\)), the Hodge spectral sequence degenerates at \(E_1\) and every \(R^jf_*\Om^i_{X/S}\) is locally free and base-change-compatible.

Example — a smooth proper family of curves

Let \(f:X\to S\) be a smooth proper family of curves, \(S\) of characteristic \(p\). Fibrewise \(\Om^{\bullet}\) lives in degrees \(0,1\), and curves always lift mod \(p^2\) — even in families, the obstruction lives in an \(H^2\) of a curve, which vanishes — so \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) is decomposable. By 4.1.5 the Hodge spectral sequence degenerates in the family and \(R^1f_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) (the relative de Rham bundle) is locally free of rank \(2g\), with \(R^1f_*\cO_X\) and \(f_*\Om^1_{X/S}\) locally free of rank \(g\) — and all three commute with base change. This is the algebraic backbone of the Gauss–Manin connection and of Serre–Tate theory: the Hodge bundle is a genuine vector bundle on the base because the family decomposes uniformly.

It is \(X'\) that must lift, not \(X\) — and \(\tF_S\) is what saves the day (Remark 4.1.6)

The sufficient condition 3.7 for decomposability asks for a lift of \(X'\) over \(\tS\), not of \(X\). Over a perfect field \(X'\cong X\) so the distinction is invisible; in a family it is real (forward-ref Lesson 14). The fix: if the Frobenius \(F_S\) of \(S\) itself lifts to \(\tF_S:\tS\to\tS\) over \(\tS\), then any lift \(\tX\) of \(X\) over \(\tS\) produces a lift of \(X'\) by base change along \(\tF_S\) — because \(X'\) is, by definition, the base change of \(X\) along \(F_S\). Such a \(\tF_S\) exists, e.g., when \(S\) is affine and smooth over a perfect field \(k\) and \(\tS\) is a lift of \(S\) over \(\Wt(k)\). So in the geometric situations one cares about, lifting \(X\) plus lifting \(F_S\) gives lifting \(X'\) for free.

Part B — Logarithmic poles (§4.2)

The log de Rham complex and the log Cartier isomorphism (4.2.1)

Let \(S\) be of characteristic \(p\), \(X/S\) smooth, and \(D\subset X\) a relative normal crossings divisor: étale-locally \(D=(t_1\cdots t_r)\) in coordinates \(t_1,\dots,t_n\). The log de Rham complex \(\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)\) is the subcomplex of \(j_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X-D/S}\) generated by \(\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}\) and the forms \(dt_i/t_i\); it is locally free with \(\Om^1(\log D)\) free on \(dt_1/t_1,\dots,dt_r/t_r,dt_{r+1},\dots,dt_n\). The whole machine descends to it.

Log Cartier isomorphism (4.2.1.1)

There is a canonical isomorphism of \(\cO_{X'}\)-modules \[ C^{-1}:\;\Om^i_{X'/S}(\log D')\;\iso\;\cH^i F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D), \] characterized, in degree \(1\), by \(C^{-1}(dt'/t')=[dt/t]\) on each branch of \(D\) and by the ordinary Cartier formula \(C^{-1}(dx')=[x^{p-1}dx]\) off \(D\). Here \(D'\subset X'\) is the (still normal-crossings) divisor obtained by base change.

There is a useful refinement. Split \(D=A+B\) into two transverse normal-crossings divisors and set \[ \Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}(A,B):=\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)(-A) \] — log poles along \(B\), honest zeros along \(A\). The natural inclusion \(\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)(-pA)\hookrightarrow\Om^{\bullet}(A,B)\) is a quasi-isomorphism (poles of order \(\leqslant p-1\) along \(A\) contribute nothing in char \(p\)), whence a Cartier isomorphism \[ \tag{4.2.1.3}\quad C^{-1}:\;\Om^i_{X'/S}(A',B')\;\iso\;\cH^i F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}(A,B). \] The case \(A=\varnothing\) is the plain log complex; \(B=\varnothing\) is the complex \(\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)(-D)\) with full zeros.

The local model, computed (4.2.2(a))

Everything reduces to the one-variable case \(X=\Aa^1_S\), \(t\) a coordinate, \(D=(t)\). There

\[ \Om^{\bullet}(\log D)=\bigl(\;\cO_S[t]\xto{d}\cO_S[t]\cdot\tfrac{dt}{t}\;\bigr),\qquad \Om^{\bullet}(\log D)(-D)=\bigl(\;t\,\cO_S[t]\xto{d}\cO_S[t]\,dt\;\bigr), \]

with \(d(t^n)=n\,t^{n-1}dt=n\,t^{n}\,\tfrac{dt}{t}\). The general normal-crossings divisor is an external tensor product of these one-variable complexes (and of the smooth-coordinate factors), exactly as in the proof of plain Cartier; multiplicativity and Künneth assemble the local models into 4.2.1.1.

Duality and topology (4.2.2(b)–(c))

Self-duality. \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)\) is self-dual with dualizing values \(\Om^n_{X'/S}(\log D')\) (\(n=\) relative dimension); the Serre dual of \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}(A,B)\) is \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}(B,A)\) — swapping which divisor carries the poles and which the zeros. This is the algebraic incarnation of Poincaré duality for the open variety.

Topology over \(\C\). There is an exact sequence \(0\to\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)(-D)\to\Om^{\bullet}\to\bigoplus_i\Om^{\bullet}_{D_i}\to\cdots\). Over \(\C\), with \(j:X-D\hookrightarrow X\), the complex \(\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)\) computes \(Rj_*\C\) while \(\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)(-D)\) computes \(j_!\C\); more generally \(\Om^{\bullet}_X(A,B)\) computes \(Rj_{A!}\,Rj_{B*}\,\C\) — extend by zero across \(A\), derived-push across \(B\). The \((A,B)\)-bookkeeping is precisely the mixed open/closed support condition.

Log variants of the whole theory (4.2.3)

Statements 2.1, 2.3, 2.4 and 3.5–3.8 all have logarithmic versions. There is an equivalence \(\Rel(X',D',\tS)\simeq\sc(\tau_{\leqslant 1}F_*\Om^{\bullet}(\log D))\) between liftings of the pair and splittings of the truncated log complex. One uses lifts \((\tX',\tD')\) with \(\tD'\) still normal crossings, with the tangent sheaf \(\Theta_{X'/S}\) replaced by the log tangent sheaf \(\Theta(-\log D')\), and one admits only Frobenius lifts \(\tF\) compatible with the divisor, namely those satisfying \(\tF^{-1}(\tD')=p\tD\). A global lift of \((X',D')\) gives a canonical decomposition of \(\tau_{<p}F_*\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)\), and a (non-canonical) full decomposition when \(\dim\leqslant p\) and \(H^{p+1}(X',(\Om^p(\log D))')=0\). Degeneration follows as before: if \(f\) is proper smooth and \(\tau_{\leqslant b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)\) is decomposable, then \(E_1^{ij}=E_\infty^{ij}\) for \(i+j\leqslant b\) (the log analogue of 4.1.4), with the parallel statement for \(\Om^{\bullet}(A,B)\). Everything transposes verbatim to algebraic spaces (4.2.5).

Corollary (4.2.4) — log degeneration in char 0

Let \(X\) be proper smooth over a field \(K\) of characteristic \(0\), and \(D=A+B\) a normal crossings divisor. Then the Hodge spectral sequence for \(\Om^{\bullet}_{X/K}(A,B)\) degenerates at \(E_1\). The case \(A=\varnothing\) reproves Deligne's classical degeneration for the log complex of an open variety.

Proof (reduction mod \(p\))

Spread \((X,D)\) out over a finitely generated \(\Z\)-algebra and reduce modulo a large prime \(p\), exactly as in Lesson 10: the reduction is proper smooth with \(D\) still normal crossings, of dimension \(<p\), and lifts (with its divisor) mod \(p^2\). The log version of 3.7 makes \(F_*\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)(-A)\) decomposable, so the log analogue of 4.1.4 gives degeneration in characteristic \(p\); a constructibility/semicontinuity argument lifts the numerical degeneration back to characteristic \(0\). No Hodge theory, no resolution of the open part. ∎

Examples

Example — the simplest log pair, \((\Pp^1,\{0,\infty\})\)

Take \(X=\Pp^1\), \(D=\{0\}+\{\infty\}\), so \(X-D=\Gm\). The global log de Rham complex \(\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)\) is, in hypercohomology, the two-term complex \(\cO_{\Pp^1}\to\Om^1(\log D)\). One computes \(H^0_{\dR}=H^0(\Pp^1,\cO)=K\) and \(H^1_{\dR}=K\cdot[dt/t]\): the form \(dt/t\) is a global log form, closed and not exact (its primitive \(\log t\) is not algebraic). So \(b_0=b_1=1\), i.e. \(H^{*}_{\dR}(\Gm/K)=K\oplus K\,[dt/t]\), matching \(\Gm\simeq\C^{*}\simeq S^1\) topologically over \(\C\). The closed/zeros complex \(\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)(-D)\) computes instead \(H^{*}_c(\Gm)\), dual to this — the self-duality 4.2.2(b) in the smallest possible case.

Example — log Cartier on \((\Aa^1,(t))\): the log form is Cartier-fixed

On \(X=\Aa^1\), \(D=(t)\), the degree-\(1\) log cohomology \(\cH^1 F_*\Om^{\bullet}(\log D)\) is free of rank \(1\) over \(\cO_{X'}\) on the class \([dt/t]\), and \[ C^{-1}\!\left(\frac{dt'}{t'}\right)=\left[\frac{dt}{t}\right]. \] The log form is its own Cartier image — the same phenomenon as for \(\Gm\) in Lesson 3. From the lift side (Lesson 6) this is forced: a divisor-compatible Frobenius lift has \(\tF^{*}(t)=t^p\cdot(\text{unit})\), so \(\tfrac1p\tF^{*}(dt/t)=d\log\) of a unit \(\equiv dt/t\) modulo exact forms.

What must lift, logarithmically

In the log theory the liftability is of the pair \((X',D')\), with \(D'\) again normal crossings, and the admissible Frobenius lifts are exactly those with \(\tF^{-1}(\tD')=p\tD\). That last condition is the divisor-level shadow of "divide by \(p\) once": it says \(\tF\) pulls the lifted divisor back to \(p\) times the divisor, which is precisely what makes \(\tfrac1p\tF^{*}(dt/t)\) land on the log generator rather than on a stray pole. Drop it and the gerbe/torsor bookkeeping of §3 no longer closes up. (Again: it is \((X',D')\), not \((X,D)\), that lifts — Lesson 14.)

The conjugate spectral sequence is the right object

Both Part A and Part B run on the same engine: decomposability of \(\tau_{\leqslant b}F_*\Om^{\bullet}\) (or its log/\((A,B)\) version) is equivalent to degeneration of the conjugate spectral sequence in total degrees \(\leqslant b\), and because the \(\cH^j\) are \(S\)-flat this is stable under base change. That single stability is what reduces the relative theorem 4.1.4 to the artinian local case 4.1.2, and what makes "degeneration in a family" mean the strong, geometric statement: locally free Hodge sheaves, formation commuting with base change.
